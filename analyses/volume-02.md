# Analyses — Volume 02

## Badger DAO — December 2, 2021 — $120M
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; front-end compromise (Cloudflare) injecting malicious approval transactions into legitimate user flows.
- Root cause: The attacker gained control of Badger's front-end (rumored via Cloudflare account/API-key compromise) and injected an `increaseAllowance()` call to their address (0x1fcdb04d0c5364fbd92c73ca8af9baa72c269107) into deposit and reward-claim transactions, so users unknowingly granted the attacker unlimited spend approval on their wBTC/ERC20 vault tokens.
- Mechanics: The malicious approvals were first inserted about 12 days before the drain; over 500 addresses eventually approved the hacker's address. Starting 00:00:23 UTC on 2 Dec 2021, the attacker used those approvals to pull tokens, including a single drain of ~900 byvWBTC worth over $50M (the victim had granted the unlimited allowance ~6 hours earlier). Most stolen assets were vault deposit tokens, which were cashed out and the underlying BTC bridged back to the Bitcoin network. Badger paused its contracts ~2h20m after the first malicious tx (using an "unusual" pausability feature in `transferFrom()`), which stopped further losses.
- Lesson: Front-end trust is transitive — scope all approvals (finite amounts, revocable) and treat every `increaseAllowance`/`approve` call that appears in a dapp's signed payloads as a potential drain; hunt for suspicious approval targets in front-end/API layers and monitor DNS/Cloudflare configuration drift.
- Aftermath: #4 on the leaderboard; detected when a user flagged the suspicious `increaseAllowance()` approval in Discord. Peckshield traced the first approvals back nearly two weeks; rekt's refrain: "infinite approval means unlimited trust".

## Beanstalk — April 18, 2022 — $181M ($76M kept)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; governance attack — flash-loaned voting power executing an emergency proposal instantly.
- Root cause: Beanstalk's governance had no delay on proposal execution (`emergencyCommit`), so a vote majority obtained via a flash loan could immediately execute a malicious proposal that transferred all protocol funds.
- Mechanics: The attacker pre-staged two governance proposals ~1 day earlier (a 1-day delay applied to all $BEAN governance actions): BIP-18 stealing all protocol assets and BIP-19 donating $250k to the Ukraine fund. Funded from Tornado via the Synapse bridge, they flash-loaned 350M DAI, 500M USDC, 150M USDT from Aave, 32.4M BEAN from Uniswap v2 and 11.6M LUSD from SushiSwap, added the stablecoins as Curve pool liquidity to farm voting power, then voted for and emergency-committed BIP-18 — draining 36,084,584 BEAN, 874,663,982 BEAN3CRV-f, 60,562,844 BEANLUSD-f and 0.54 UNI-V2 WETH/BEAN. After removing liquidity and repaying the flash loans, profit was 24,830 WETH (~$76M), laundered via Tornado Cash.
- Lesson: Governance weight must be un-flash-loanable (locked/vote-escrowed tokens with decay) and every governance action needs a timelock — audit `emergency*` paths for instant execution and for init/upgrade proposals that can move all treasury funds.
- Aftermath: #5 on the leaderboard (rekt records the $181M damage, not the kept profit); traced step-by-step by Peckshield, Igor Igamberdiev and Kelvin Fichter. The anon founder Publius doxxed as a group of three afterward to deflect insider-job suspicion; Omniscia noted the attack fell outside their audit scope.

## bEarnFi — May 16, 2021 — $18M
- Chain/Attack vector: Binance Smart Chain; unit/denomination mismatch between vault and strategy withdrawal logic, amplified with a CREAM flash loan.
- Root cause: `BvaultsBank.withdraw()` interpreted the withdrawal amount as BUSD while `BvaultsStrategy` interpreted the same number as ibBUSD (an interest-bearing token worth more than BUSD), so each withdrawal credited the user ~2.7% more value than they deposited.
- Mechanics: The attacker flash-loaned 7,804,239 BUSD from CREAM and deposited it into BvaultsBank, which routed to BvaultsStrategy and the Alpaca vault, minting 7,598,066 ibBUSD (farmed via Alpaca FairLaunch). Withdrawing the full 7,804,239 amount was interpreted by the strategy as 7,804,239 ibBUSD = 8,016,006 BUSD of value. Repeating the deposit/withdraw cycle kept inflating the user's credit until the pool was drained; the attacker repaid the flash loan with 7,806,580 BUSD and kept the profit.
- Lesson: Cross-contract calls must use consistent asset denominations — an interest-bearing share token (ibToken) passed through an interface that expects the underlying is a classic credit-inflation bug; hunt every bank/strategy boundary for missing exchange-rate conversion.
- Aftermath: Weekend attack (10:36 AM UTC, May 16, 2021); analysis from Peckshield and bEarn. Rekt's meta-lesson: BSC's copy-paste code boom makes forked protocols easy prey — "time is the most valuable audit of all".

## Belt Finance — May 29, 2021 — $6.3M
- Chain/Attack vector: Binance Smart Chain; flash-loan manipulation of strategy share valuation (incorrect share pricing in bVenusBUSD/bEllipsisBUSD vaults).
- Root cause: Belt's vault strategies valued shares off current pool balances, letting an attacker deposit into the most-undersubscribed strategy (shares priced cheap) and withdraw from the most-oversubscribed one (shares priced rich) after swinging the underlying price with flash-loaned liquidity — the same incorrect-share-valuation family as Harvest Finance.
- Mechanics: The attacker took 8 flash loans totaling $385M BUSD from PancakeSwap; deposited 10M BUSD into the bEllipsisBUSD strategy and 187M BUSD into bVenusBUSD (both undersubscribed). They swapped 190M BUSD → 169M USDT through Ellipsis to skew the pool, withdrew more BUSD than deposited from the now-oversubscribed bVenusBUSD strategy, then swapped 169M USDT → 189M BUSD; steps 4–7 were repeated 7 times. Flash loans were repaid and the profit withdrawn.
- Lesson: Share valuation derived from spot AMM balances is manipulable — require time-weighted/oracle-resistant pricing; the deposit-cheap/withdraw-rich "undersubscribed vs oversubscribed strategy" loop is a recognizable BSC farm-exploit signature to hunt for in vault code.
- Aftermath: Analyzed by FrankResearcher and Mudit__Gupta; rekt stresses the similarity to the $25M Harvest Finance hack and Belt's collaboration announcement two days earlier. EPS LP/stakers also profited from the incident.

## Bondly Finance — July 15, 2021 — $5.9M
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; infinite mint via a compromised (or insider-controlled) mint address.
- Root cause: The BONDLY token's mint address (0x58a058ca4b1b2b183077e830bc929b5eb0d3330c) was used to mint 373M new BONDLY — the minting privilege sat behind a single key/wallet with no cap, multisig or timelock, and was either compromised or exercised by the team itself.
- Mechanics: The attacker minted 373M BONDLY and dumped them 100,000 at a time, crashing the BONDLY price by ~80%. ~$4.8M DAI was sent to Tornado Cash; ~$1.1M in DAI and BONDLY remained on the associated wallet (0xc433d50dd0614c81ee314289ec82aa63710d25e8) at the time of writing.
- Lesson: Any mint function behind a single EOA key is a rug/compromise vector — hunt for uncapped mints, minter roles without multisig/timelock, and large mints immediately followed by small repeated dumps to AMMs and Tornado.
- Aftermath: Team's announcement tweet claimed the wallet "was compromised by an unknown party"; rekt draws a parallel to the Paid Network rug. Bondly had ~47k Twitter followers, Binance investment and celebrity partnerships (Logan Paul; Jake Paul was hit by the Chainswap exploit days earlier) — first entry on the leaderboard at #28.
