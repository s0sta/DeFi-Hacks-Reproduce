## Warp Finance — 2020-12-18 — $7.8M
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; flash-loan price manipulation of Uniswap V2 LP tokens used as lending collateral (flash-swapped $180M from Uniswap, flash-borrowed $51M from dYdX).
- Root cause: Warp Finance priced collateral from a spot Uniswap-based oracle (WarpOracle 0x4A224CD0…) on LP token prices. One large WETH→DAI swap roughly doubled the WETH-DAI LP token price (58,815,427 → 135,470,392), doubling the computed collateral value and letting the attacker borrow ~2x what the collateral was really worth.
- Mechanics: An account funded with just 1 ETH via Tornado Cash took four flashloans (2.9M DAI + 76k WETH from dYdX; 90k/82k/96k WETH from Uniswap pairs), deposited 2.9M DAI + 76k WETH to mint 94,349 WETH-DAI LP tokens as collateral, then swapped 341k WETH for 47.6M DAI on Uniswap to make DAI expensive and pump the LP token price. Against the inflated collateral the attacker borrowed 3.86M DAI + 3.9M USDC (~$7.8M), repaid the flashloans, and exited. Notably the attacker did not profit immediately: the LP collateral stayed locked in an underwater position and most gains were eaten by fees — at write time the funds remained recoverable via liquidation.
- Lesson: Never value LP-token collateral from spot AMM prices — a single large swap in the underlying pair doubles the LP token's recorded value; require TWAP or manipulation-resistant pricing for any collateral whose value is a function of pool composition. Also note the "deposit LP → pump underlying → borrow against inflated collateral" flow as a recognizable flash-loan lending signature.
- Aftermath: Attack initiated 22:24 UTC on Dec 17, 2020, traced step-by-step by rekt's OPSEC; pie chart credited to @n2ckchong. Rekt mused that a future "altruistic attacker" might use the same technique to ransom funds back to protocols, Jekyll-and-Hyde framing included.

## Wasabi Protocol — 2026-04-30 — $5.9M
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum + Base + Berachain + Blast; private-key compromise of the sole deployer EOA holding ADMIN_ROLE over every upgradeable vault, then UUPS upgrade to a malicious implementation and a fake-strategy drain. "No vulnerability was found and no code was broken."
- Root cause: wasabideployer.eth (0x5c629f8c…) held unchecked ADMIN_ROLE across all upgradeable vaults with no multisig, no timelock, no governance. The attacker called `grantRole()` with delay=0, becoming admin instantaneously, then upgraded WasabiVault proxies and the LongPool via UUPS to prepared malicious implementations; `strategyDeposit()` accepted any destination with no whitelist and no post-call check, so a minimal fake strategy contract with a `drain()` function swept everything.
- Mechanics: Funding arrived from a Tornado-linked address at 01:12 UTC Apr 30; malicious contracts were deployed and gas loaded by 07:46; at 07:49:47 the Ethereum drain emptied eight vaults in one transaction (840.9 WETH ~$1.9M, ~$172K USDC, 400B REKT, 878M PEPE, 10B MOG, etc.), Base followed 2m50s later, the LongPool at 07:59:35. Totals: ~$2M Ethereum, ~$2.5M Base, plus Berachain/Blast → $5.9M across four chains. Tokens were converted to ETH and split across five attacker wallets; four of five moved through Tornado Cash within days. Blockaid flagged the drain live; CertiK followed within minutes.
- Lesson: A single EOA holding ADMIN_ROLE over upgradeable vaults is a standing full-drain primitive — in any audit, enumerate who can upgrade + route assets and demand multisig/timelock or flag it as critical. `strategyDeposit`-style admin asset-routing functions without a destination whitelist convert a key compromise into a direct sweep of every vault.
- Aftermath: Wasabi's first statement came 2+ hours after Blockaid's alert with no figure or mechanism; Virtuals Protocol (50+ AI trading agents on Wasabi) froze margin deposits faster than the team's second post; ZachXBT's question — "Why did a single EOA seemingly have so much control?" April 2026 closed as DeFi's worst month (~$635M across 28→29 incidents).

## WazirX — 2024-07-18 — $235M
- Chain/Attack vector: CEX Safe multisig compromise (Ethereum hot wallet 0x27fD43BA…); hybrid attack — two private keys compromised, two signers phished into signing a malicious multisig *upgrade* while believing they were approving a routine USDT transfer.
- Root cause: WazirX's 6-signer Safe (5 WazirX, 1 Liminal custody provider) was upgraded to a malicious implementation using 2 compromised keys + 2 phished signatures; the phished signers' wallets displayed a normal USDT transfer while the payload was the upgrade (WazirX suspects a display-vs-content discrepancy in Liminal's interface). One of the phished signatures was Liminal's — a verification failure in the custody co-signer's final checks. A legitimate USDT transfer that failed minutes before the drain went unnoticed.
- Mechanics: Preparation began ≥8 days before (Tornado-funded test transactions Jul 8–10 involving ETH, SHIB and USDT with the multisig). On Jul 18 the attacker upgraded the Safe to their contract and drained: 33M USDT (22M TRON + 11M ETH), $18.6M BTC plus $14M BTC on TRON, $5M USDC, and more, swapping tokens to ETH on the way out. Cyvers alerted during the drain; WazirX confirmed ~30 minutes later and paused withdrawals; the attacker's KYC'd exchange deposit was later identified via an Arkham bounty (ZachXBT). #7 on the leaderboard.
- Lesson: Multisig UX is part of the security model — signers must verify the *decoded transaction contents*, not the wallet's display, and custody co-signers must independently verify payloads (a display/payload mismatch is a known attack class). Failed or anomalous transactions immediately preceding a drain are precursors worth alerting on. "Not your keys, not your crypto" applies to exchanges whose Safe is the crown jewel.
- Aftermath: Mudit Gupta's breakdown calls it "a very methodical and organized attack, pointing towards DPRK as the hacker" (unconfirmed); rekt notes the pattern of CEX multisig compromises after DMM Bitcoin's $304M in May 2024.

## Whale Hunter's Payday — 2024-08-20 — $55.47M (DAI)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; phishing — a whale signed a malicious transaction handing control of their EOA to the attacker, who then took over the victim's DSProxy and re-owned their Maker vault.
- Root cause: No code flaw — the victim unknowingly signed a phishing transaction that gave the attacker control of the externally owned account. From there the attacker transferred ownership of the victim's DSProxy (a delegated multi-call contract) to their own address, changed the Maker vault's owner, and withdrew everything. The ownership-transfer semantics of DSProxy meant EOA control = vault control.
- Mechanics: On Aug 20 ZachXBT noticed 55.47M DAI leave a single wallet; the whale attempted to withdraw their funds but the ownership change had already executed and the transaction failed. The attacker withdrew 55,473,618 DAI directly to 0x5D4b2A02… (main heist tx 0xf70042bf…); by reporting time 27.5M DAI had been swapped for 10,625 ETH. Victim 0xf2B88943…, phishing address 0x0000db5c….
- Lesson: For any wallet using a DSProxy (or similar delegated proxy), signing a single malicious transaction transfers *everything* the proxy controls — monitor whales' proxy-owner changes and failed owner-change withdrawals as live-phishing telemetry. Phishing remains the highest-ROI "exploit": CertiK reported ~$498M lost to it in H1 2024 alone.
- Aftermath: Blocksec's Jingyi Guo inferred the phishing vector from the victim's failed DSProxy invocations after the ownership change; rekt's closer — "there's always a bigger phish out there and it might just be waiting for you to take the bait."

## Wintermute (I) — 2022-06-05 — 20M OP (~$27.6M)
- Chain/Attack vector: Optimism; deployment-replay takeover — 20M OP were sent to a Gnosis Safe proxy address that had no contract deployed on Optimism; an attacker replayed the proxy deployment to make themselves owner.
- Root cause: Wintermute handed the Optimism Foundation its *Ethereum* multisig address for an Optimism distribution and confirmed receipt of 1 OP and 1M OP test transactions without verifying access. Gnosis Safe's 2020-era ProxyFactory uses the CREATE opcode (address depends on factory address + nonce, not CREATE2 salt), so anyone could replay deployments on Optimism until the nonce matched the mainnet deployment and a matching proxy address was created — with the attacker as owner.
- Mechanics: 20M OP were sent May 27; Wintermute realized the error May 30, but the OP launch went ahead Jun 1 with ~10% of circulating supply unowned. An exploiter (Tornado-funded Jun 1) deployed an adapted ProxyFactory and replayed batched deployments of 162 safes at a time until the matching address was created, hijacking the funds Jun 5. They sold 1M OP for 720 ETH and sent 1M OP to Vitalik's address; 18M OP remained unsold at write time. The Optimism Foundation issued Wintermute a replacement 20M OP.
- Lesson: Always deploy the receiving Safe (or any counterfactual contract) on the target chain before a large distribution, and treat "confirmed receipt" as worthless unless the recipient verified *access*. Legacy CREATE-based factories are cross-chain replayable — audit factory nonce/address determinism when contracts or funds are expected at an address that only exists on another chain.
- Aftermath: The exploiter waited 4 days between deploying the contract and hijacking (pointed out by yoav.eth) — rekt wonders why an attacker would leave Wintermute a rescue window; the 20M OP stake (almost 10% of circulating supply) raised governance-takeover concerns.

## Wintermute (II) — 2022-09-20 — ~$160M ($162.3M at write time)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; private-key compromise of a Profanity-generated vanity-address hot wallet, used as admin to drain the firm's DeFi vault.
- Root cause: Both the hot wallet and vault used vanity addresses with leading zeros generated by the Profanity tool, whose key generation is brute-forceable (the flaw was raised on GitHub in January 2022 and publicly disclosed the week before the hack). Wintermute had removed ETH from the admin address after the disclosure but *forgot to remove the address as admin from the vault*, so the compromised key still controlled the vault.
- Mechanics: The attacker (0xe74b28c2…) used the recovered key to drain ~$162.3M: $118.4M of stables (mostly parked into Curve 3pool to avoid blacklisting — making the attacker the 3rd-largest 3CRV holder at 13% of supply), 671 WBTC (~$13M), 6,928 ETH ($9.4M), and small-caps including 21% of PRIMATE's supply. CEO Evgeny Gaevoy announced the theft ~3 hours later, stating Cefi/OTC were unaffected and the firm remained solvent. In the days after the Profanity disclosure, 0x6AE09A… drained $3.3M from other vanity wallets.
- Lesson: Vanity-address wallets are a weak-key risk class — inventory privileged addresses and check key-generation provenance (Profanity-era addresses are assumed-compromised). When a key is suspected compromised, revoke *all* of its roles and approvals, not just move its funds; Wintermute's missed admin removal is the entire lesson. First major hack after the Tornado Cash sanctions.
- Aftermath: The vanity address was chosen for "gas savings" per Gaevoy — an expensive choice; a scammer capitalized on watchers by spoofing a "WinterMuteInu" honeypot from the exploiter's address (~166 ETH captured). Rekt flags post-sanction 3pool-as-mixer concerns for Curve users.

## WooFi — 2024-03-05 — $8.5M
- Chain/Attack vector: Arbitrum; flash-loan manipulation of WOOFi's sPMM oracle price for WOO, then draining the WooPPV2 pool via three swaps.
- Root cause: WOOFi v2's sPMM adjusts oracle prices based on trade value to manage slippage, but an error let a price adjustment land outside the expected range — and the fallback check against Chainlink did not cover the WOO token's price. The recently added WOO lending market on Arbitrum plus thin WOO liquidity made the manipulation economically feasible.
- Mechanics: The attacker (0x9961190b…) flash-loaned funds, manipulated the sPMM's WOO price, and drained the pool using the swap function 3 times, taking ~$8.5M (attack tx 0x40e1b8c7…, contract 0xD4c633C9…). Spreek broke the news; the team paused the affected pools within 13 minutes. WOOFi sent an on-chain message assuming a whitehat and offering a 10% bounty; their post-mortem confirmed the out-of-range adjustment and the missing WOO fallback. CertiK had audited the swap/oracle contracts in October 2022.
- Lesson: Novel oracle designs (sPMM) must range-bound every price adjustment and have a fallback that covers *every* token, especially the protocol's own token — a native token with a new lending market and thin liquidity is the classic economics for this attack. Multi-chain expansion re-opens per-chain economic assumptions; re-audit the oracle per deployment.
- Aftermath: Part of Arbitrum's early-2024 spree (Radiant Capital, Gamma Strategies, Seneca before it); rekt mocks the "One DEX to rule all chains" slogan and the team bragging about its sPMM hours before the exploit.

## WOO X — 2025-07-24 — $14M
- Chain/Attack vector: CEX hot-wallet drain across five networks (Ethereum, Bitcoin, BNB, Arbitrum, TRON); targeted phishing of a team member's device → development-environment access → hot-wallet control. No smart-contract flaw.
- Root cause: A team member's device was compromised by phishing, granting the attacker access to WOO X's development environment and ultimately the ability to orchestrate withdrawals directly from hot wallets across five chains — an ops/endpoint-hygiene failure. The exchange's "best-in-class security" (ISO/IEC 27001, Fireblocks, cold-storage claims) did nothing to stop it.
- Mechanics: Jul 24 ~14:09 UTC WOO X announced a "contained incident" while funds were already flowing: the ETH hot wallet 0x63DFE4e3… to 4 attacker addresses, the BTC hot wallet bc1qm4hy… to 5 addresses, 5.03 BTCB on BSC, and ~7M TRX on TRON (traced by Cyvers, who sent rekt the trace). Nine user accounts were cited by the team, but on-chain data shows hot-wallet outflows directly to attackers; the tally climbed from ~$12M to $14M confirmed. Compensation came from the "company treasury."
- Lesson: A single phished employee device = multi-chain hot-wallet drain; CEX audits should treat dev-environment access, signing flows and withdrawal review as the primary attack surface, not the smart contracts. Watch for the "user accounts were breached, treasury compensates" narrative pattern that papers over hot-wallet losses.
- Aftermath: Third WOO-ecosystem incident in two years (Kronos Research $26M API-key theft Nov 2023; WooFi $8.5M oracle exploit Mar 2024); the bug bounty program was quietly paused on Bugcrowd two weeks before the breach. Rekt: security theater — "all flash, zero substance."

## Wormhole — 2022-02-02 — $326M (120k wETH)
- Chain/Attack vector: Solana bridge; forged VAA SignatureSet → minted 120k wETH (whETH) on Solana without any backing, then bridged most of it back to Ethereum.
- Root cause: `verify_signatures` delegated signature verification to a Secp256k1 program, but a discrepancy between `solana_program::sysvar::instructions` and the `solana_program` version Wormhole was using meant the contract did not correctly verify the address supplied; the attacker used a SignatureSet created in a previous transaction (with an account holding just 0.1 ETH) to bypass the guardians and call `complete_wrapped`, fraudulently minting 120k whETH against a VAA verification from an earlier transaction.
- Mechanics: The attacker (0x629e7da2…) created an account hours earlier containing a single serialized Secp256k1 instruction, faked the SignatureSet, and minted 120k wETH. 93,750 ETH were bridged back to Ethereum in 3 transactions (still in the hacker's wallet at write time); the remaining ~36k whETH were liquidated on Solana into USDC/SOL. samczsun flagged the issue within minutes; the team first claimed "down for maintenance", then promised and restored 1:1 backing within 24 hours. Certus One's on-chain $10M whitehat bounty offer went unanswered.
- Lesson: Bridge mint primitives must verify the *actual guardian signatures and address*, not merely delegate to a precompile — audit the instruction-verification boundary (sysvar vs program-version mismatch) and every path where a previously-created VAA/SignatureSet can be replayed into a fresh mint. A $326M loss from one mis-verified address is the whole lesson.
- Aftermath: Second bridge exploit in less than a week (after Qubit) and second on the leaderboard; rekt notes Vitalik Buterin's cross-chain security warnings being validated, and that the $10M bounty was the biggest ever offered.

## xToken (I) — 2021-05-12 — $24M+ (2.4k ETH, 781k BNT, 407k SNX)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; flash-loan price manipulation of the SNX spot price used when minting xSNXa (pricing routed via Kyber, which routed to Uniswap v2), executed through a private Flashbots transaction.
- Root cause: The protocol's buy price for SNX (used to price xSNXa mints) flowed through Kyber → Uniswap v2, so the attacker could crush the on-chain SNX spot price and mint xSNXa for a fraction of its real value — while the protocol's internal valuation of xSNXa remained normal, making the minted tokens swappable at the uncrushed price.
- Mechanics: dYdX flash loan of 61,833 ETH (~$267M): deposited 10k ETH to borrow 564k SNX on Aave and swapped 5.5k ETH → 700k SNX on Sushi; sold 1.2M SNX for 818 ETH on Uniswap v2, crashing SNX; minted 1.2B xSNXa for just 0.12 ETH (the protocol bought SNX at the crushed price via Kyber→Uni v2); swapped 105M xSNX → 414 ETH at the normal internal price; reversed the swaps and repaid Aave/dYdX; then issued xBNTa four times for 0.03 ETH total (3.9B xBNTa) and swapped half for 781k BNT. Everything was sold to ~5.6k ETH via 1inch.
- Lesson: When a protocol mints at market price but values internally at a "normal" price, the gap is pure arbitrage — audit every pricing hop (here Kyber→Uniswap) and require manipulation-resistant feeds for mint/valuation. Flash-loan + private-transaction (Flashbots) combinations mean mempool monitoring will miss the attack; rely on on-chain post-hoc monitoring instead.
- Aftermath: The fourth leaderboard entry for auditor Peckshield ("most rekt auditor"); rekt notes the shock that a "blue chip" with strong partnerships fell, and openly wonders whether the attack came from within.

## xToken (II) — 2021-08-29 — ~$4.5M (xSNX)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; access-control typo — an erroneous `require()` made xSNXAdmin's `callFunction` publicly callable, enabling debt-burn/swap manipulation with a dYdX flash loan.
- Root cause: `callFunction` on xSNXAdmin (which burns outstanding sUSD debt and swaps SNX for sUSD during rebalances) was meant to be callable only by dYdX's SoloMargin flashloan contract. The code used `require(sender==address(this))` instead of `require(msg.sender==soloMarginAddress)`, so anyone could call it and swap the contract's SNX at a manipulated spot price.
- Mechanics: Flash loan of 25,000 ETH from dYdX; borrowed ~1M SNX from Aave V1+V2; swapped 6.8k ETH → 519k SNX on Bancor (holding ~1.5M SNX); dumped 1.5M SNX on Kyber for ~6.5M USDC, depressing SNX; swapped USDC → sUSD on Curve; transferred ~2M sUSD to xSNXAdmin and called `callFunction` to burn the sUSD debt and swap 614k SNX for 811k sUSD at the artificially depressed price; swapped sUSD → USDC, reversed everything and repaid. ~$4.5M stolen — same token, same flash-loan technique, three months after the first hit.
- Lesson: Privileged functions need exact access-control checks — grep every `require()` for typos and wrong comparison operands (sender vs msg.sender, == vs !=); a debt-management function that swaps at spot prices is a manipulation surface even when access control is *meant* to exist. A protocol hit twice by the same family in one quarter needs a whole-family re-audit, not a patch.
- Aftermath: The team compensated users again and phased out xSNX, admitting it was "by far our most complex product" — rekt notes the reputation damage of two leaderboard entries and the implicit confession of complexity risk.

## Yearn (I) — 2021-02-04 — $11M vault loss ($2.7M attacker profit)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; nine flash loans funding an arbitrage loop against the yDAI v1 vault during a migration window in which the withdrawal fee had been turned off.
- Root cause: The vault's withdrawal fee was disabled for the migration, making deposit/withdraw free; combined with a 3CRV pool that the attacker skewed by swapping, each deposit/withdraw cycle against the pool's changing composition let the attacker extract value — the vault's share price moved with the skewed pool while deposits/withdrawals remained fee-free.
- Mechanics: Flash loans of 116k ETH (dYdX) + 99k ETH (Aave v2); borrowed 134M USDC + 129M DAI on Compound; added 134M USDC + 36M DAI to the 3CRV pool and withdrew 165M USDT; then repeated five times: deposit 93M DAI (less each time) into the yDAI vault, add 165M USDT to 3CRV, withdraw 92M DAI from the vault, withdraw 165M USDT from 3CRV; the final round withdrew 39M DAI + 134M USDC. Each cycle left the attacker with more 3CRV, later swapped to stables. The $11M loss split: $2.7M attacker profit, $3.5M to Curve LPs, $3.5M to Curve stakers, $1.4M in Aave v2 fees.
- Lesson: Migration "fee-off" windows convert a vault into a free arbitrage machine if its pricing pool can be skewed — audit migration states and treat zero-fee + skewed-pool combinations as exploitable by construction. Vault share prices derived from spot AMM balances are manipulable with deposits/withdrawals alone (no fancy calls needed).
- Aftermath: The Yearn post-mortem did not mention flash loans; Igor Igamberdiev published the detailed flow. Tether froze 1.7M USDT of the loot. Rekt debunked the Cronje-vs-Bouteloup feud theory — the attacker's account was funded hours before the criticism was made.

## Yearn (II) — 2023-04-13 — $10M+ (yUSDT iearn vault)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; misconfiguration in the immutable iearn yUSDT contract — the Fulcrum *USDC* address had been used instead of Fulcrum USDT since launch — exploited to mint 1.2 quadrillion yUSDT for 10k USDT.
- Root cause: The legacy yUSDT token (deployed 1,156 days earlier, in the Andre Cronje/iearn era) contained a copy/paste error in its yield basket: it tracked Fulcrum USDC instead of Fulcrum USDT. The attacker manipulated the underlying share prices of yUSDT and minted a vast quantity with trivial collateral.
- Mechanics: Using the misconfigured basket, the attacker minted ~1.2 quadrillion yUSDT with just 10k USDT and swapped them for other stables totaling $11.4M (BlockSec's breakdown). The attacker was Tornado-funded and re-deposited 1,000 ETH for laundering; the three exploiter addresses held ~$1.5M + $1.5M + 7.4M DAI at write time. Team member storming0x acknowledged the attack before official comms; current vaults (~$450M TVL) were unaffected.
- Lesson: Yield-basket configuration (strategy/token addresses) is security-critical state — a wrong address in a basket is a mint primitive; hunt for copy/paste token-address errors in legacy contracts. Immutable legacy contracts still hold TVL years after deprecation and cannot be patched — they need monitoring, exit paths, or explicit wind-down, not neglect.
- Aftermath: CertiK's February 2020 audit of iearn covered only yDAI; 1,156 days passed before anyone noticed. Rekt ties it to "test in prod" culture and notes Sushi was hit the same week.

## Yearn (III) — 2025-11-30 — $9M (yETH stableswap)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; arithmetic underflow in the Newton-Raphson solver of Yearn's forgotten yETH stableswap — a corrupted invariant plus a dust deposit minted 2.35×10⁵⁶ yETH and drained the pool in one atomic transaction.
- Root cause: yETH was a separate, unmaintained product with novel custom math (no shared code with V2/V3 vaults). The attacker corrupted the pool invariant with `remove_liquidity(0)` calls, `update_rates()` calls, OETH rebasing and the rounding asymmetry between pow_up/pow_down, driving the state to Σ=0, Π=0, supply=0. Then a dust deposit (1 wei each of wstETH, rETH, cbETH, 9 wei mETH) triggered `calc_supply()`; with amplification A=4.5×10²⁰ and the impossible Σ/Π relationship, the term (A·Σ − s·r) went negative and wrapped to ~10⁷⁷, so the solver "converged" on D_new ≈ 2.3544×10⁵⁶ — minted straight to the attacker.
- Mechanics: 21:11 UTC Nov 30, a single transaction: ~$8M drained from the yETH stableswap pool and ~$0.9M from the Curve yETH-WETH pool; ~1,000 ETH (~$3M) went into Tornado Cash as ten 100-ETH deposits in the same block; helper contracts self-destructed. Seed funds arrived via Railgun 30 minutes before. Yearn's war room (SEAL911 + ChainSecurity) and Plume/Dinero recovered $2.33M (857.49 pxETH); the primary attacker address still held ~$3.84M.
- Lesson: Abandoned-but-live contracts with novel math are the highest-ROI hunt targets of 2025–26 — no maintainer, no monitoring, full value. Specifically audit Newton-Raphson solvers and invariant math for underflow when zero-amount operations and rate updates can corrupt Σ/Π state; "legacy code isn't technical debt, it's a loaded gun."
- Aftermath: Togbe spotted unusual Tornado/LST movements first; Peckshield alerted; Banteg's post-mortem confirmed the unchecked underflow. Rekt's irony: a casual researcher found what established processes didn't — "which legacy products need eyes on them?"

## Yearn (IV) — 2025-12-16 — ~$293K (yTUSD)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; a virtual carbon copy of the April 2023 yUSDT exploit on the 2,100-day-old iearn TUSD vault — the strategy tracked the wrong asset (Fulcrum iSUSD/sUSD) while the vault's underlying was TUSD.
- Root cause: Same misconfiguration class as the $10M 2023 exploit: the vault's strategy was set to track Fulcrum iSUSD (holding sUSD) instead of TUSD. A donation attack broke the share accounting — donating assets the vault was blind to crushed the denominator to near-zero, so minting yTUSD shares produced "extreme supply inflation" for the cost of a rounding error.
- Mechanics: With a 30M USDC Morpho flash loan the attacker donated blind assets, broke the share accounting, minted an astronomical quantity of yTUSD shares, and dumped them into the Curve yPool for yDAI/yUSDC — draining the pool and collapsing the yPool LP price. Profit ~$293K, swapped to ~103 ETH parked at 0x0F214a04… (no Tornado, no bridges). Downstream collateral damage: STABLEx, which prices collateral off the Curve yPool, faced theoretical insolvency; ~214k sUSD remain permanently stuck in the vault because the old code doesn't recognize sUSD as a valid asset.
- Lesson: The "strategy tracks the wrong asset" misconfiguration is a repeatable, copy-pasteable exploit class — inventory every legacy vault's strategy-address configuration. Donation attacks against broken share accounting are the standard archaeology toolkit. Immutable = unguarded: attackers are now systematically excavating five-year-old bytecode — treat locked liquidity in ancient contracts as hacker bounties, not savings.
- Aftermath: William Li caught it live ("Another Yearn V1 vault attack is underway!"); Yearn's response was an archeological disclaimer ("deployed over 2,100 days ago"). Rekt frames a season of "relic hunting": Abracadabra's deprecated cauldrons ($1.8M), Balancer's rounding error ($128M), and Yearn's own yETH ($9M) two weeks earlier.

## YieldBlox — 2026-02-22 — $10.97M
- Chain/Attack vector: Stellar (Blend V2 lending); oracle manipulation — one 100x trade in a dead USTRY/USDC SDEX market poisoned the Reflector VWAP oracle, and the pool lent $10.97M against 100x-inflated collateral.
- Root cause: Four stacked failures, none a contract bug: (1) USTRY — a yield-bearing US-Treasury stablebond — was listed as collateral while its SDEX market had <$1 hourly volume and <5 tokens on the ask side; (2) Reflector's VWAP oracle pulled directly from SDEX trades, so in a dead market one trade *is* the VWAP; (3) the Oracle Adapter returned the latest price with no median or deviation check; (4) the protocol had no circuit breaker, staleness flag or market-quality gate. The health-factor check worked exactly as formally verified — against a poisoned input.
- Mechanics: The attacker's wallet was created Feb 14 (56.32 XLM); on Feb 21 23:35 a burner account placed a sell offer of 1.2185 USTRY at 107 USDC (100x); at 00:10:21 Feb 22 a trigger account bought 0.05 USTRY against it — a ~50-cent trade that became the oracle's price at 00:15/00:20. Depositing ~153k USTRY (real value ~$160k, oracle value up to $15.99M), the attacker borrowed 1,000,196 USDC + 61,249,278 XLM in two transactions. Funds were swapped to USDC and bridged via Allbridge → Base → Ethereum (Across/Relay); Tier 1 Validators froze ~48M XLM (~80%); a 72-hour 90%-return bounty from the Security Council was ignored, and funds exited via Tornado.
- Lesson: Collateral listings need market-quality gates — minimum depth, multi-maker, volume floors — because a formally verified health factor is worthless if the oracle input is one 50-cent trade; audit the whole oracle→adapter→pool pipeline for median/deviation/staleness checks. No audit can cover "should we list this asset?" — that's governance, and governance must ask whether the collateral's market can be trusted.
- Aftermath: Blend V2 ran a $125k Code4rena + first-ever Rust/Soroban Certora formal verification contest (Feb 2025) — every proof held; the exploit lived between the code and the world (same architecture as Makina Finance a month earlier). Script3 compensated depositors; gas wallets were funded by flagged phishing addresses and one EVM wallet carries a Binance KYC trail.

## ZeroLend — 2025-02-23 (disclosed late 2025) — ~$371K (3.92 LBTC)
- Chain/Attack vector: Base; collateral-manipulation borrow — PT-LBTC (a Pendle principal token) supplied as collateral with gameable oracle parameters, borrowed LBTC, bridged the profit out. ZeroLend kept it secret for ten months.
- Root cause: A risk-management decision, not a code bug: ZeroLend listed PT-LBTC — a discount-trading Pendle derivative — as borrowable collateral with oracle parameters that could be gamed, eighteen days after Ionic Money proved LBTC derivatives were being hunted with the same playbook (a fake LBTC token social-engineered into a listing). No audit covers collateral-listing decisions; that is governance.
- Mechanics: Feb 23, 2025, the attacker (0x218C572b…, funded via Across) deposited PT-LBTC as collateral and borrowed 3.923 LBTC in three transactions within 45 minutes (0.9532, 1.4769, 1.4929); swapped the LBTC through Aerodrome and bridged the profit out via Across — arrived with 38 ETH, left with 163.65 ETH (~$125k net). The debt token still accrues interest on-chain; the worthless PT-LBTC collateral sits untouched. ZeroLend never disclosed: Discord blamed "high utilization" and "maintenance" until torakapa's tweet in late 2025.
- Lesson: After a public precedent (Ionic's $8.8M, 18 days earlier), listing the same asset class with gameable oracle parameters is a governance failure — audit new collateral listings against recent incidents in the same asset family. Hunt "silent insolvency": zombie markets where supply works but withdrawals return "high utilization" forever — and check for automated siphoning bots draining whatever liquidity appears.
- Aftermath: A Gnosis Safe (created two months after the exploit) with Gelato automation has extracted $100k+ from the broken pool since — "someone built an automated extraction operation on top of a pool ZeroLend left broken and open." ZERO token -100%, OKX delisting, GitHub flatlined since Sept 2025, while the founder's LinkedIn still claims "$200M+ under management."

## ZKasino — 2024-04-20 — $33M rug
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; exit-scam rugpull — 10,500 ETH of user deposits converted to ZKAS at an inflated price without consent, then staked on Lido under a 15-month vesting schedule that was never disclosed.
- Root cause: A trust-based raise with no escrow and a fraudulent team: users were promised 1:1 ETH withdrawal after farming ZKAS, but the platform instead converted 10,500 ETH (~$33M) into ZKAS at an inflated valuation and locked it in Lido staking with the *investors'* vesting terms (15% TGE, 3-month cliff, 15-month linear) imposed on users. The "zk" and "EigenDA" tech claims were false — the "ZKasino chain" was an Arbitrum Nitro chain deployed in two minutes.
- Mechanics: The bridge page (later deleted) had stated funds would be "returned and can be bridged back" once the chain was live; instead the ETH was swapped into ZKAS and staked on Lido. Red flags had been piling up since December 2023: ZachXBT publicly flagged the founder and team (unpaid Pancakesbrah, a $200K giveaway never awarded, an unpaid bet, gore-video marketing, unreimbursed phishing victims); IDOs on ApeTerminal and AIT Protocol were abruptly canceled; Telegram mods gaslit investors ("People always FUD"). The team then went dark, banning critics.
- Lesson: Exit-scam signatures compound: canceled listings + unpaid contractors + false tech claims + a founder with failed-project history + investor vesting imposed on user funds — when a "chain" is a 2-minute Arbitrum Nitro deployment and deposits aren't 1:1 redeemable, treat the raise as a rug target. "If ZachXBT is calling something a scam months in advance, run to the exits."
- Aftermath: Founder Ildar "Derivatives Ape" Elham came from the failed ZigZagExchange/Syncus projects (allegedly misusing ZigZag's $15M raise to build ZKasino); Series A investors ($26M at a $350M valuation — MEXC, Big Brain Holdings, Trading Axe, Pentoshi, Sisyphus) had the very vesting terms later imposed on users; Big Brain Holdings declined its token distribution and distanced itself.

## zkLend — 2025-02-11 — $9.57M
- Chain/Attack vector: Starknet; rounding-error/precision manipulation of the `lending_accumulator` — flash-loan loops inflated it to 4.069297906051644020, turning a 1-wei deposit into free withdrawals.
- Root cause: Three "individually harmless" design details combined lethally: empty-market deposits, accepted "donations", and withdrawal rounding. The accumulator's precision allowed the attacker to deposit 4.069297906051644021 wstETH, receive 2 wei back, and withdraw 6.103946859077466029 wstETH — spending just 1 wei in the process. Nethermind had audited twice (Cairo 0 in 2022, Cairo 1 in 2023) with "no critical issues" — the arithmetic still bit.
- Mechanics: The attacker's custom contract ran flash-loan transactions that squeezed `lending_accumulator` from 1.0 to 851.0 in a single transaction, landing on the magic value 4.069297906051644020, then used the rounding to mint free tokens and drained 2,200+ ETH plus USDC, STRK and USDT — $9.57M total. 1,801 ETH were bridged to Ethereum (0x645c7783…); 706.4 WETH sent to Railgun were *bounced back* by its exclusion policy. A 10% whitehat offer with a Feb 14 deadline went unanswered; the post-mortem arrived via an unverified Google Drive link and claimed FBI/HSI involvement.
- Lesson: Lending-accumulator precision is an exploit surface — model deposit/withdraw rounding at extreme accumulator values and empty-market states; "empty market + donations + withdrawal rounding" is a known killer combination across lending forks. Audits certify code as written, not arithmetic under adversarial precision — add property tests for share/exchange-rate rounding.
- Aftermath: SlowMist linked the attacker on-chain to the EraLend reentrancy exploit (July 2023) via two shared L1 addresses — a repeat operator (zkLend's post-mortem also names OnyxDAO, Yei, Channels, Starlay, though those claims lack evidence). Vitalik praised Railgun's rejection as "privacy pools done right."

## Zoth — 2025-03-21 — $8.4M
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; admin-key compromise → proxy upgrade of `USD0PPSubVaultUpgradeable` to a malicious implementation → 8.85M USD0++ withdrawn, swapped to DAI, and moved within minutes.
- Root cause: The deployer/admin wallet was compromised (stolen credentials); with admin authority the attacker upgraded the vault proxy and drained it — "no complex financial wizardry required, just god-mode admin access and stolen credentials." Three weeks earlier (Mar 1) Zoth lost $285k to a Uniswap V3 manipulation of its LTV-validation logic (minting ZeUSD undercollateralized) — a validation weakness SolidityScan had publicly flagged as still open; the lesson didn't stick.
- Mechanics: Mar 21 the attacker (0x3b33c5Cd…) upgraded the vault proxy and, within minutes of the upgrade, withdrew 8.85M USD0++ tokens ($8.4M), converted to DAI, and moved them to 0x7b0cd0D8… (attack tx 0x33bf669d…). John Doe caught it live; SlowMist and Cyvers confirmed; asset issuers locked down 73% of Zoth's TVL right after, limiting further damage. Zoth + Securr offered a $500k/10% bounty; Crystal Blockchain took over the investigation. The attacker had stalked the protocol for weeks, funding wallets and deploying contracts in multiple failed attempts.
- Lesson: Whoever holds upgrade rights on a vault proxy holds a full-drain primitive — audit admin/upgrade custody (multisig? timelock?) on every proxy, and treat a second exploit within weeks as evidence the first post-mortem's fixes never landed: verify remediation, not announcements. Admin-key compromise on an RWA protocol is indistinguishable from insider action.
- Aftermath: Rekt: "No contract audit in existence could have saved Zoth from its $8.4 million digital execution" — the humans holding the keys were the problem; "protocols should start auditing the people who work for them."

## Zunami (I) — 2023-08-13 — $2.1M (1,184 ETH)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; price manipulation of Zunami's zETH and UZD Curve pools via flash-loan swaps (e.g. SDT on Sushiswap), exploiting a flawed price calculation in `totalHoldings`.
- Root cause: The protocol computed LP-token prices from `totalHoldings` — the pool's current balances — which large swaps and slippage could skew. The attacker manipulated the LP token prices of the zETH/UZD pools and drained them, depegging zETH by 85% and UZD by 99%.
- Mechanics: Using flash loans the attacker executed large token swaps (SDT) to create slippage, manipulated the LP prices, and drained the two pools for 1,184 ETH (~$2.1M), deposited straight into Tornado Cash. Attacker 0x5f4c21c9…; exploit txs 0x2aec4fdb… (zETH) and 0x0788ba22… (UZD). Peckshield raised the alarm (deliberately withholding tx hashes after the BlockSec/Vyper-debacle backlash); BlockSec published the step-by-step. The collateral itself remained secure per the team.
- Lesson: Any valuation function that reads raw pool balances (`totalHoldings`) is donation/slippage-manipulable — hunt for it in yield aggregators and stake-DAO strategies. Curve-ecosystem contagion in summer 2023 (Conic, JPEG'd, Alchemix, Curve itself, then Zunami) shows that when one ecosystem protocol falls, every adjacent protocol should be re-audited immediately.
- Aftermath: The attacked MimCurveStakeDAO strategy was added after Ackee Blockchain's audit of an earlier version (the article was edited to remove the Ackee reference); the team's first statement claimed "collateral remain secure" mid-drain. Rekt credits the post-Vyper era's new alerting standards and the SEAL 911 hotline as the (imperfect) community response.

## Zunami (II) — 2025-05-14 — ~$500K
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; admin-key compromise (or insider job) — a single call to `withdrawStuckToken()` drained the UsdtCrvUsdStakeDaoCurve strategy of 296,456 LP tokens. "No flash loans. No price manipulation."
- Root cause: The vault's emergency `withdrawStuckToken()` function could sweep the entire strategy's collateral for whoever held the admin key — and the key was either compromised or the "compromise" was the plan. The abandonment signals were neon: no GitHub commits for 3+ months, TVL bleeding for months, an expired domain, and Curve gauge rewards dropping to 0% *right before* the exploit. Michael Egorov: "What is worse, admin key existed!"
- Mechanics: On May 14 the Zunami deployer wallet (0xe9b2B067…) granted admin role to 0x05137041…; seven minutes later that address called `withdrawStuckToken()` on the UsdtCrvUsdStakeDaoCurve strategy and transferred 296,456 LP tokens — the collateral for zunUSD and zunETH (~$500K) — to itself, then into Tornado Cash. Fourth Zunami incident after the Jan 2023 $49K sandwich, the Feb 2023 $260K flashloan series, and the Aug 2023 $2.1M price manipulation.
- Lesson: Emergency withdraw functions are the most dangerous permission in any contract — they need multisig + timelock + per-asset limits, and any protocol where one key can `withdrawStuckToken` the whole vault is a standing drain. When an "admin key compromise" hits a protocol showing every abandonment symptom (no commits, gauge rewards zeroed, domain expired), treat insider exit as the leading hypothesis. First Discord response "rekt lmfao" is the tell.
- Aftermath: CEO Kirill Kozlov surfaced two weeks later with "compromised deployer or malicious intent by the key holder"; CTO Mikhail Zelenin blamed Russian border police cloning his laptop and denied owning a Ferrari; even a team moderator threatened to file a Thai police report (deadline passed unanswered). Vladimir S. flagged the compromise; PeckShield confirmed; rekt: "Emergency functions make excellent getaway vehicles."
