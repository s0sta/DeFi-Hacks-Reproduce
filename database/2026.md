# Database — 2026

## MT Token
- 2026-01 / BSC / fee-logic (unbounded fee split)
- Mechanics: MT's `transactionFee()` splits `transactFeeValue` across an unbounded list of percentages without enforcing `sum(shares) <= 100`, so a transfer can debit the sender for far more than `amount`. AMM pairs become unintended fee targets. Attacker flashloans USDT, buys MT, seeds MT into the MT/USDT pair, then `skim()` + `sync()` to lock manipulated reserves and swaps a small MT amount to drain ~36,995 USDT from the pair.
- Lesson: When auditing transfer-fee tokens, prove that fee percentages are bounded and sum to ≤100%, and that contracts (esp. AMM pairs) cannot be overcharged into a reserve desync.

## PRXVT
- 2026-01 / Base / reward-accounting logic flaw
- Mechanics: Attacker stakes 400,000 PRXVT, then loops (until a gas threshold) transferring the whole stPRXVT balance into a freshly CREATE2-deployed helper which calls `earned()` + `claimReward()` and returns the shares. Because the staking contract's reward debt is not carried per-holder correctly, each fresh address can claim the accrued rewards again, repeatedly harvesting ~32.8 ETH worth of PRXVT.
- Lesson: Check that staking rewards track per-account reward debt updated on every transfer/claim; transferring shares to a fresh address must never reset claimable rewards.

## SynapLogic
- 2026-01 / Base / input-validation (bulk refund accounting)
- Mechanics: The presale's `buy()`/`swap()` accept arrays of `recipients`, `rates`, and `refundFlags` and pay each entry a `rate`% refund out of the contract's balance, while `msg.value` (or the swap input) is received only once. The attacker passes dozens of entries all flagged for a 10% refund, so total refunds vastly exceed the single payment and drain the sale contract of ~27.6 ETH and ~3,450 USDC.
- Lesson: Any bulk/array parameter path that distributes value must verify `sum(distributed) <= received` in the same units before any transfer.

## Truebit
- 2026-01 / Ethereum / arithmetic-overflow in bonding-curve pricing
- Mechanics: The TRU pool's `getPurchasePrice` uses a quadratic formula; the PoC reverse-solves the buy amount `A` that drives the formula to the uint256 maximum (via `maxPrice/(100*R)` + sqrt), where the computed price overflows/loses precision and the purchase is effectively mispriced. The attacker loops buy → sell of the same TRU amount, each iteration netting ETH from the pool, until ~8,540 ETH is drained.
- Lesson: Bonding-curve and AMM pricing formulas need explicit overflow/precision bounds — test buy/sell symmetry at extreme amounts and add revert guards near max values.

## Futureswap
- 2026-01 / Arbitrum / fee unit-mismatch (logic flaw)
- Mechanics: The victim contract computes a token-unit fee (`abs(delta) * feeRateWad / 1e18`) and forwards it into `FeeManager.addFee()`, where the receiving system interprets the value as feeBasisPoints. The attacker seeds positions with tiny/big deltas, runs `updateFunding()`, then a main `changePosition(-68 WETH, 496,500 USDC)` and a drain `changePosition(0, -894,992 USDC, 0)` to collect ~394,742 USDC.e plus WETH from victim inventory.
- Lesson: When cross-contract calls pass numeric values, verify the units/scaling each side expects — a wad-vs-bps mismatch turns fees into free withdrawals.

## Makina
- 2026-01 / Ethereum / oracle manipulation (pool-balance pricing)
- Mechanics: Makina's `Machine.updateTotalAum()` prices DUSD from Curve pool balances via `accountForPosition` commands (`balanceOf` reads). With a 280M USDC flashloan the attacker pumps DUSD and MIM in their Curve pools, calls the accounting/update functions to make the protocol see DUSD as expensive, then swaps the cheaply-bought DUSD (and LP) back to USDC at the inflated valuation, netting ~5.1M USDC. Repeated twice in one tx.
- Lesson: Never price protocol assets from raw AMM `balanceOf` values that a flashloan can move in one block; use TWAPs or manipulation-resistant oracles.

## LAXO Token
- 2026-02 / BSC / fee-on-transfer + reserve desync
- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans 350 BUSD from Pancake V3, buys LAXO via the fee-on-transfer router path, adds and then removes liquidity, then raw-transfers their entire LAXO balance directly into the BUSD pair so its real balance diverges from stored reserves, and executes a low-level `pair.swap()` to extract BUSD at an inflated rate (~137K USD profit). The flawed fee accounting on LAXO transfers is what makes the pair's balances/reserves exploitable.
- Lesson: Fee-on-transfer tokens interacting with V2 pairs should be fuzzed for balance-vs-reserve drift; raw token donations into a pair followed by a direct `swap()` is a red flag worth testing on every new pair.

## Moonwell
- 2026-02 / Base / oracle manipulation → bad debt
- Mechanics: With a WETH flashloan from an Aerodrome CL pool, the attacker calls `liquidateBorrow(victim, amount, mcbETH)` to repay a victim's WETH debt and seize cbETH collateral, redeems mcbETH for cbETH, swaps cbETH→WETH, and repays — profiting from the liquidation discount. The incident's root cause is the Moonwell Chainlink cbETH oracle on Base mispricing collateral, allowing underwater positions to be liquidated in a way that left ~1.78M in protocol bad debt.
- Lesson: Collateral pricing feeds derived from thin/exchange-rate markets must be cross-checked and deviation-bounded, or liquidations become a free money printer for the first caller.

## Alkemi Earn
- 2026-03 / Ethereum / self-liquidation logic flaw
- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans 51 WETH, supplies 50 ETH as aweth collateral, borrows 39.5 aweth against it, then calls `liquidateBorrow{value: amount}(self, aweth, aweth, amount)` paying exactly their own borrow balance and finally `withdraw(aweth, max)` — the borrow/liquidate/withdraw accounting lets a borrower capture the liquidation value of their own position, netting 43.45 ETH from the protocol.
- Lesson: Liquidation must be zero-sum for self-liquidations — enforce borrower≠liquidator or deduct seized collateral from the borrower's balance before paying out.

## Curve LlamaLend / crvUSD
- 2026-03 / Ethereum / access-control (public vault mint) + liquidation gaming
- Mechanics: Using nested Morpho flashloans (USDC then WETH) the attacker creates a crvUSD loan, deposits into the Yearn scrvUSD vault, freely calls `sDOLA.mint()` to mint shares out of thin air, and uses a second contract to iterate `crvUSD_Controller.users_to_liquidate()` and `liquidate(user, 0)` on every liquidatable position. Redeeming the inflated shares and pocketing liquidation proceeds nets ~$240K before repaying.
- Lesson: ERC4626-style vaults must restrict minting to the deposit path and liquidations must respect collateral math — a public mint plus a permissionless liquidate-all loop is an open drain.

## EST / BNBDeposit
- 2026-03 / BSC / accounting desync (skim loop) + EIP-7702 EOA delegation
- Mechanics: Using EIP-7702 delegation (needed because BNBDeposit only lets EOAs claim), the attacker flashloans 20,000 WBNB, repeatedly deposits into BNBDeposit, swaps WBNB→EST, sends 1 EST directly to BNBDeposit to desync its internal accounting, then runs a 100-iteration loop of transferring EST into the pair and `pair.skim(bnbDeposit)` to keep inflating the deposit contract's balance. The desynced accounting credits disproportionate LP/withdrawable value, and the attacker swaps EST back for a ~150.2 WBNB profit.
- Lesson: Deposit contracts that derive user value from raw token balances must be audited for direct-donation and skim() desync paths; also test EIP-7702 delegation against EOA-only gating.

## Venus (THE market)
- 2026-03 / BSC / access-control (stolen approvals) + donation-inflation + borrowBehalf
- Mechanics: Six EOAs had pre-approved the future attack-contract address, letting the attacker `transferFrom` their THE into vTHE — inflating the market exchange rate. The attacker then uses Venus `borrowBehalf(victim, ...)` to take USDC, CAKE and WBNB loans on the victim's behalf (cash to attacker, debt to victim), mints vUSDC, enters the market, borrows THE and donates it back into vTHE to further inflate collateral value for another borrowBehalf round.
- Lesson: Track allowance grants to undeployed/predicted addresses as a hunting signal, and flag `borrowBehalf`-style functions where collateral valuation can be pushed by direct donations.

## MONA LisaVault
- 2026-04 / BSC / business-logic abuse (self-referral) + insider LP drain
- Mechanics: Each 220 USDT node purchase pays 70 USDT to the L1 referrer and 50 USDT to L2. The attacker deploys 25 fresh `NodeBuyer` proxy contracts (bypassing the 1-node-per-address cap) that register the attack contract as L1 and an attacker-controlled L2, recouping 120/220 USDT per node. Stage 1 (insider component) has the vault owner remove their 100%-owned MONA/USDT LP, routing ~66,450 USDT to the exploit contract; total profit ≈60,950 USDT.
- Lesson: Referral rewards must be self-referral-proof (no cycles, no same-wallet tiers) and per-address caps must be bypass-proof; check LP ownership concentration for insider-drain paths.

## Saturn Protocol (sUSDat)
- 2026-04 / Ethereum / arithmetic underflow (DoS) + tolerance-bound extraction
- Mechanics: SAT-001: after `transferInRewards()` and `burnQueuedShares()` the invariant `strcBalance >= getUnvestedAmount()` breaks, so `convertFromStrc()` underflows (`strcBalance - unvested`) and panics (0x11), freezing all withdrawals for up to 30 days with no admin escape hatch. SAT-002: `_validateConversion()` applies toleranceBps=2000 (20%) independently to both price and amount, so the PROCESSOR role can extract up to 1-(0.8/1.2) = 33.33% per `convertFromUsdat()` call. TVL at risk ~$35.7M (disclosure, unpatched).
- Lesson: Audit vesting/redemption arithmetic for state sequences that break `balance >= unvested` invariants, and never stack independent tolerance windows on both sides of a conversion.

## DxSale
- 2026-05 / BSC / access-control (owner backdoor)
- Mechanics: The DxSale Legacy Liquidity Locker's owner-only `DXLOCKERLP(address,uint256)` backdoor drains any locked LP position without respecting individual timelocks. Ownership was secretly relayed through 89 wallets over 269 days to the attacker, who used an EIP-7702 delegated drainer contract to batch-empty all 1,400+ locked LP positions for ~$7.3M in BNB.
- Lesson: An owner-only function that moves user-locked funds IS the vulnerability; verify lockers enforce timelocks inside the token transfer path, not just at the UI, and watch ownership-transfer chains.

## Joe Agent
- 2026-05 / BSC / reentrancy (CEI violation)
- Mechanics: `removeLiquidityViaContract(liquidity,...)` sends BNB to the caller before zeroing `lpInfo[user].lpAmount`. The attacker parks one ~437 LP position, then its `receive()` re-enters with the same liquidity value 25 times — every re-entry passes the stale `liquidity <= lpInfo[user].lpAmount` check and burns a fresh slice of the pool's shared LP, draining 62.5 BNB + ~1.19M JOE.
- Lesson: State updates that gate asset accounting (lpAmount, shares, allowances) must happen before any external call; reentrancy via `receive()` on BNB/ETH payouts is the classic missed path.

## LegendaryMoneyMonNft
- 2026-05 / BSC / access-control (ecrecover zero-address signature bypass)
- Mechanics: `cliamRewred()` verifies a claim via `recoverSigner(hash, sig) == admin`, but the admin was set to address(0) and `ecrecover` returns address(0) for malformed signatures without the contract checking it. A forged 65-byte signature (r=0, s=0, v=27) therefore verifies, and the attacker drains the contract's entire MON balance, swapping it for ~85.5K USDT.
- Lesson: `ecrecover` returns address(0) on failure — always require the recovered signer != address(0), and treat an admin set to zero as an immediate drain condition.

## SKP
- 2026-05 / BSC / access-control (owner backdoor) + oracle manipulation
- Mechanics: The SKP token has an owner-only `ownerBurnLiquidityPairTokens(amount)` that burns SKP sitting directly inside the SKP/USDT pair. The owner burns ~95% of the pair's SKP, then `pair.sync()` re-bases reserves, leaving USDT intact and spiking the on-chain SKP price ~20x. The owner then supplies the over-valued SKP as collateral on Venus/Lista DAO and borrows BTCB + USDT (~$212K).
- Lesson: Any owner function able to move/burn tokens inside an LP pair is a price-oracle weapon against lending markets that price by spot reserves.

## SKP (exit-scam framing) — SKP_exp2.sol
- 2026-05 / BSC / malicious token hook (engineered rug)
- Mechanics: SKP's `_transfer()` fires `_runSpecialPairFlow()` when the pair sends SKP to a whitelisted buyer, redistributing an unbounded amount of treasury SKP to the single owner-set WL_ADDRESS; the trigger (`balanceOf(pair) - reserve`) is flash-loan inflatable. The attacker (same tx as SKP_exp) buys with ~205M flashloaned USDT routed to WL, triggering a ~9.7B free-SKP treasury dump, then sells the free SKP back to drain ~205M USDT from the pair — an operator-engineered rug (WL set 6 days prior, unverified source, throwaway token pattern).
- Lesson: Unverified token source + owner-set whitelist + pair-triggered mint/redistribution hook is the rug-pull fingerprint; treat "hacks" of fresh tokens as operator exfiltration until proven otherwise.

## WUSD / GLOVE
- 2026-05 / Ethereum / Sybil incentive-abuse (no claim ledger)
- Mechanics: `WUSD.wrap()`'s `_englove()` mints ~2 GLOVE to any caller whose current GLOVE balance is under 2e18 — eligibility keys only on the live balance, with no per-address claim ledger, cooldown, or identity binding. The attacker flashloans USDT, spins up 80 fresh helper contracts that each wrap 100k WUSD (advancing the global epoch), pumps 101 extra wrap/unwrap cycles to complete vesting, then unwraps and dumps the free GLOVE into thin Uniswap V3 GLO/USDC and GLO/USDT pools, draining the LPs.
- Lesson: Reward/incentive systems must track per-address claims and vesting in storage — any reward conditioned only on current balance is Sybil-farmable by fresh addresses.

## YSDAO
- 2026-05 / BSC / access-control (permissionless sync) + tax-bypass logic
- Mechanics: YSDAO's transfer protection detects add/remove-liquidity by comparing pair balances to reserves. The attacker buys YSDAO while having the pair pay out 1 wei USDT so `_isRemoveLiquidity()` reads true and buy taxes/cooldown are skipped; then calls the unprotected `Staking.sync()` which donates all staking-held USDT into the pair and resyncs reserves, raising the apparent YSDAO price; then transfers 1 USDT into the pair before selling so the sell takes the `_isAddLiquidity()` (untaxed) branch. Profit ~19.5K USDT.
- Lesson: Tax/branch logic driven by pair-balance-vs-reserve comparisons is gameable with 1-wei donations — verify staking `sync()`/donation entry points are access-controlled and that fee branches don't depend on attacker-influenced balance deltas.

## ATM
- 2026-06 / BSC / Sybil bypass of anti-whale + auto-dump logic flaw
- Mechanics: ATM's `_transfer()` sell branch auto-sells 20% of the sell size from the contract's own reserves at `amountOutMin=0` whenever its balance exceeds `swapAtAmount`, leaking value to marketing/dividend wallets. All anti-whale guards (MAX_HOLDER, coldTime, tOwnedU profit fee) key off fresh per-address state, so the attacker spreads 2.85M ATM across 30 CREATE2 farmer clones (each under the cap, zero cooldown, zero cost-basis), pumps the price via a burn-on-buy ratchet, then dumps all 30 clones to extract ~243,543 USDT (net ~89K) from the pair.
- Lesson: Per-address rate limits with fresh-address defaults of zero are not limits — any Sybil-able guard must key off global/totalized state, and contract self-balance auto-sells are a leak worth modeling in every trade.

## BY Token
- 2026-06 / BSC / access-control (permissionless burn) + price manipulation
- Mechanics: `triggerAutoBurn()` is permissionless once trading is enabled. The attacker corner-buys BY via the router (output routed to the router itself), donates WBNB to the pair and `sync()`s to cross the trading-enable BNB threshold, buys BY for themselves, then calls `triggerAutoBurn()` to burn BY out of the pair — crashing the BY reserve and spiking the price. Five 1-wei sells plus a final dump of the remaining BY drain ~146.6 WBNB (~$87.4K) at the manipulated price.
- Lesson: Token burns that hit LP-pair holdings must be restricted and delayed, not permissionless — a burn plus a sync is a one-transaction price pump against spot-priced exits.
