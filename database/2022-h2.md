# Database — 2022-h2

## Audius
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-07 / Ethereum / access-control (unguarded initializer) → governance takeover
- Mechanics: The governance proxy's `initialize()` was callable by anyone (no init guard), so the attacker re-initialized it with votingPeriod=3 blocks, executionDelay=0 and themselves as guardian. They then submitted a proposal to `transfer` 99% of the governance treasury's AUDIO to their contract, self-delegated stake via `delegateStake` to satisfy quorum, voted Yes, executed, and swapped the AUDIO for ~704 ETH.
- Lesson: Unguarded initializers on governance proxies are full treasury takeovers — always require `initialized` state checks on every config entry point.

## FlippazOne
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-07 / Ethereum / access-control
- Mechanics: The NFT bidding game's `ownerWithdrawAllTo()` lacked an `onlyOwner` modifier, so any caller could drain the contract. The PoC bids 2 ETH and then calls `ownerWithdrawAllTo(attacker)` to sweep the entire ETH balance.
- Lesson: Grep every withdraw/rescue function for a missing access-control modifier — the cheapest bugs are plain missing `onlyOwner`.

## LPC
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-07 / BSC / token-logic (mint-on-self-transfer) + flashloan
- Mechanics: The attacker flash-swaps the PancakeSwap pair's entire LPC reserve, then self-transfers the balance 10 times in a loop; each self-transfer triggers the token's flawed transfer logic that credits the sender extra tokens. They repay the flash loan (with a 10% fee) and dump the inflated LPC for ~178 BNB.
- Lesson: Self-transfers on fee-on-transfer/reward tokens must be a no-op; test transfer(own address, x) in isolation.

## Omni
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-07 / Ethereum / reentrancy (ERC721 callback) in NFT lending
- Mechanics: The Lib helper supplies 3 Doodles as collateral and borrows WETH from the Omni pool; when the pool's `withdrawERC721` calls `safeTransferFrom`, the attacker's `onERC721Received` hook re-enters to liquidate and re-supply the same NFTs (with `useAsCollateral=true`) while the health-factor check still sees stale state. They repeat borrows against the same 20 NFTs, redeem the vault's WETH and unwind via Balancer/Doodles flash loans.
- Lesson: ERC721 `onERC721Received` is a reentrancy vector — health checks and collateral accounting must be re-entrancy-safe around NFT transfers.

## Quixotic
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-09/2022-07 (dir) / Optimism / access-control (missing buyer authorization)
- Mechanics: `fillSellOrder` only validated the seller's signature (`_validateSellerSignature`) and not the buyer's authorization, so the attacker could pass an arbitrary `buyer` address and have the marketplace pull that buyer's tokens to settle their own sell order.
- Lesson: NFT marketplace fills must verify both sides — seller signature AND buyer consent/approval — before moving funds.

## SpaceGodzilla
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-07 / BSC / oracle/reserve manipulation via public liquify functions
- Mechanics: With ~2.95M USDT flashloaned from 16 pools, the attacker calls the token's permissionless `swapTokensForOther` and `swapAndLiquifyStepv1`, donates tokens to the LP pair and executes large swaps that skew the pair reserves, then sells back the pumped SpaceGodzilla for USDT (25,378 BUSD profit).
- Lesson: Public "swapAndLiquify" tokenomics entry points let anyone re-shape pool reserves — never price anything off a token's auto-liquify loop.

## ANCH
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / BSC / fee-on-transfer + skim manipulation
- Mechanics: Flashloan 50k USDT from DODO, buy ANCH, then donate the ANCH to its pair and call `Pair.skim(address(Pair))` 60 times; each skim transfer triggers ANCH's fee-on-transfer logic that inflates the pair's ANCH balance. A final `skim(attacker)` extracts the excess and the attacker sells ANCH back for more USDT than borrowed.
- Lesson: `skim`/`sync` are permissionless — combined with fee-on-transfer tokens they become price-manipulation primitives.

## Circle (USDC vault) — Circle_exp1.sol
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / Ethereum / access-control (CDP allowance) + flashloan
- Mechanics: A Maker Vault owner had previously granted the attacker's contract CDP authority (`cdpAllow`). The attacker flashloans DAI, joins it to the vault urn, then `frob`s with negative dink/dart (1/4 of the debt/collateral) using that delegated authority, `flux`es the UNI-V2 LP collateral out, burns the LP for DAI/USDC and sells the USDC.
- Lesson: Token/CDP allowances granted to third-party contracts are live attack surface — track and revoke them like approvals.

## Circle (USDC vault) — Circle_exp2.sol
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / Ethereum / access-control (CDP allowance) + flashloan
- Mechanics: Same incident, second tx: the attacker flashloans a larger DAI amount and this time zeroes out the entire urn (`dink = -urn.ink`, `dart = -urn.art`), fluxing all LP collateral out, burning it and selling the USDC for ~$151K.
- Lesson: A compromised CDP allowance enables complete vault liquidation — monitor unusual `frob`/`flux` activity on vaults.

## DDC
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / BSC / access-control (fee handler)
- Mechanics: The token's `handleDeductFee(actionType, feeAmount, from, user)` was public and let anyone pull "fees" from an arbitrary `from` address. The attacker buys DDC, calls `handleDeductFee(0, pairReserve-1, TargetPair, attacker)` to move the pair's DDC to themselves, then `sync()`s and sells into USDT.
- Lesson: Fee-deduction handlers must restrict `from` to legitimate sources — a public "deduct from anyone" is a drain.

## EGD Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / BSC / oracle manipulation (reward price off pair reserves) + flashloan
- Mechanics: After staking 100 USDT, the attacker flashloans 2000 USDT and flash-swaps 99.99999925% of the EGD/USDT pair's USDT, crashing the `getEGDPrice()` used for reward claims. `claimAllReward()` then pays out EGD priced at the manipulated (inflated) EGD/USDT rate, and the attacker dumps the EGD to repay.
- Lesson: Staking reward claims priced from live pair reserves can be pumped and farmed within one flashloan tx.

## ETN Product
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / BSC / reward/business-logic manipulation + flashloan
- Mechanics: Flashloan 9400 BUSD; the attacker swaps to WBNB, mints an ETN NFT, self-invites into the Etnshop (commId 11), mints a shop and creates a product, then donates 600k U to its pair and burns LP — each step interacts with reward logic that overpays, and finally `Market.saleU` converts the inflated U into profit before repaying the loan.
- Lesson: Invite/mint/product reward flows with attacker-chosen IDs and pair donations are farmable — trace every reward math path.

## Lucky Tiger
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / Ethereum / weak randomness
- Mechanics: The NFT mint's `getRandom()` uses `keccak256(abi.encodePacked(block.difficulty, block.timestamp)) % 2`, which the attacker computes off-chain and re-creates by warping to the winning timestamp, minting 10 NFTs at 0.01 ETH each whenever the parity check favors them.
- Lesson: Block-derived randomness is predictable by anyone in the mempool — use Chainlink VRF for lottery logic.

## Nomad Bridge
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / Ethereum / bridge (message verification via uninitialized trusted root)
- Mechanics: After Nomad's Replica was mistakenly initialized with a trusted root that is provable without a real merkle proof, any attacker can replay a legitimate cross-chain transfer message (100 WBTC from Moonbeam) with the recipient field swapped to their own address, and `Replica.process` accepts it.
- Lesson: Verify initialization state of cross-chain verifiers — a mis-set trusted root turns the bridge into an open faucet.

## Qixi
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / BSC / tokenomics (dividend-on-transfer) + flashswap
- Mechanics: The attacker flash-swaps essentially all WBNB out of the QIXI pair, then in the callback transfers ~1e15 QIXI to the pair, which triggers QIXI's transfer-fee/dividend logic that credits the attacker during the reserve imbalance, and repays the flash swap with profit.
- Lesson: Dividend-distribution code inside `transfer` executes in pair context during flash swaps — model it as state-changing, not a view.

## Reaper Farm
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / Fantom / access-control (missing allowance check in redeem)
- Mechanics: `ReaperVaultV2.redeem(shares, receiver, owner)` never checks that `msg.sender` is the owner or has an allowance; the attacker simply calls `redeem(victim's shares, attacker, victim)` and receives the victim's entire USDC share of the vault (~$1.7M).
- Lesson: ERC-4626-style `redeem(owner)` must enforce `owner == msg.sender || allowance` — test cross-account redemption.

## XST
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / Ethereum / fee-on-transfer + skim/reserve manipulation
- Mechanics: Flash-swap WETH from the WETH/USDT pair, swap into XST on the WETH/XST pair, donate XST back and spam `skim()` 15 times so each skim transfer triggers XST's fee-on-transfer burn on the pair, skewing its reserves, then swap XST back to WETH at the inflated rate and refund the flash swap.
- Lesson: Repeated `skim` calls on fee-on-transfer tokens burn/redistribute pair balances — audit fee logic for transfer-frequency side effects.

## XST — XST_exp2.sol
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-08 / Ethereum / fee-on-transfer + skim/reserve manipulation
- Mechanics: Cleaner rewrite of the same XST exploit: flash-swap 2× the pair's WETH, sell WETH→XST, donate XST/8 to the pair, loop 15 `skim(pair)` calls, final `skim(attacker)`, then sell all XST back to WETH at the inflated price and repay with a 0.3% fee.
- Lesson: Same transferable lesson as XST_exp.sol: skim loops on fee-on-transfer tokens are a manipulation primitive.

## BNB48 MEV Bot
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-09 / BSC / access-control (unauthenticated callback)
- Mechanics: The MEV bot's public `pancakeCall` accepts attacker-crafted `data` whose first bytes encode the recipient the bot should pay; the attacker calls it directly four times (once per token) with themselves encoded as recipient and drains the bot's USDT, WBNB, BUSD and USDC.

## BXH
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-09 / BSC / logic flaw (deposit(0,0) balance-based accounting) + flashloan
- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans 3.17M USDT, swaps most into BXH to pump its pair, donates 805k USDT to the staking contract, and calls `deposit(0, 0)` through the delegate caller path — the zero-amount deposit records a stake based on the contract's USDT balance, then they drain the staking contract's USDT and dump BXH back.

## Bad Guys by RPF
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-09 / Ethereum / access-control (missing whitelist amount check)
- Mechanics: `WhiteListMint(merkleProof, chosenAmount)` validated only the merkle proof for whitelist membership and never checked the amount against the leaf data; the attacker supplied a valid proof and minted 400 NFTs in one call.

## DPC

## Nereus NXUSD

## New Free DAO
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-09 / BSC / reward-math (fresh-address timer reset) + flashloan
- Mechanics: The reward contract's claim (0x6811e3b9) pays out based on time since the caller's `owner_d` timestamp, which defaults in a way that fresh addresses get a large first payout; the attacker spawns 50 new contracts, sends each the full NFD balance, and every one claims a full reward, minting NFD repeatedly, then dumps for WBNB (4481 BNB total).
- Lesson: Per-address first-claim defaults that reset for fresh addresses are farmable via contract factories — snapshot total minted, not per-address time.

## RADT
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-09 / BSC / access-control (wrap withdraw from arbitrary `from`) + fee-on-transfer
- Mechanics: The attacker sends 1 USDT to the pair, then calls `wrap.withdraw(0x68Db…, pair, pairRADT * 100/9)` — the wrap contract pulls RADT out of an arbitrary `from` address and deposits it to the pair, triggering RADT's 9%-burn-on-transfer, after which `sync()` raises the price and the attacker sells RADT for USDT at the inflated rate.

## ROI (Ragnarok)
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-09 / BSC / access-control (ownership + fee setters) + reflection accounting
- Mechanics: `transferOwnership` lacked a check, so the attacker becomes owner, zeroes fees, excludes all holders from rewards, donates ROI to the pair, sets a 99% tax, flashloans ROI from the pair (the 99% tax burns the pair's tokens), repays, then calls `includeInReward(self)` which resets `_tOwned` to 0 while `_rOwned` remains, letting them sell tokens they no longer "own".
- Lesson: Reflection tokens — audit `includeInReward`/`excludeFromReward` transitions for rOwned/tOwned mismatches and always guard ownership transfer.

## Shadowfi

- Mechanics: `SDF.burn(address, uint256)` is public; the attacker buys SDF, calls `burn(TargetPair, pairBalance - 1)` to torch the pair's SDF reserve, calls `sync()`, and sells their SDF at the now-inflated price for WBNB.

## Thunder Brawl
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-09 / BSC / weak randomness + reentrant double-claim
- Mechanics: `shoot`/`claimReward` derive winners from a sha256 of attacker-supplied `_x/name/_add` values (precomputable), and `claimReward` is re-entered from `onERC721Received` while the house wallet still holds ≥2× the amount, paying the reward twice per game.

## BEGO

## Carrot

## UERII
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-10 / Ethereum / access-control (public mint)
- Mechanics: `UERII.mint()` has no access control, so the attacker mints a large amount and swaps it for USDC then WETH via Uniswap V3 (~$2.5k).
- Lesson: Check every mint function for `onlyOwner` — an unguarded mint is instant value extraction.

## VTF
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-10 / BSC / reward logic (fresh-address claims) + flashloan
- Mechanics: The attacker deploys 400 `claimReward` contracts via create2, flashloans 100k USDT, buys VTF and transfers it to the first contract, then chains `claim()` calls through all 400 — each fresh address's `updateUserBalance` grants a full first-time reward, multiplying the payout, which is finally sold back for USDT.
- Lesson: Fresh-address reward resets are farmable in bulk with factories — bound rewards by total stake, not per-address timestamps.

## Xave Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-10 / Ethereum / governance (unpermissioned DAO module + self-answered oracle)
- Mechanics: The SafeNap DAO module lets anyone `addProposal` and then answer the Reality.eth question themselves (`submitAnswer` with a 1-wei bond, no arbiter); the attacker proposes a mint of 100T RNBW plus `transferOwnership` of RNBW/LPOP/PrimaryBridge and executes all four tx hashes.
- Lesson: DAO modules where the proposer can also answer the oracle are governance bypasses — separate proposer, oracle and executor roles.

## AUR
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / access-control (public reward setters)
- Mechanics: `changeNodePrice` and `changeRewardPerNode` on the AurumNodePool are public; the attacker sets both to astronomical values, creates a node, rolls/warps time forward and claims the inflated node reward, then dumps AUR for BNB.
- Lesson: Node-reward rate and price setters must be owner-only — public setters turn rewards into faucets.

## Annex
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / access-control (liquidation helper executes attacker swaps)
- Mechanics: The attacker deploys a worthless token, adds its LP with 8 flashloaned WBNB, then flash-swaps WBNB into the Annex `Liquidator` contract, whose logic (triggered by the swap) grants the attacker a transferFrom; the attacker pulls the liquidator's WBNB balance and removes liquidity.
- Lesson: Liquidation helpers that react to attacker-supplied pair swaps can be coerced into transferring out their own funds.

## BDEX
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / logic (public harvest on skewed reserves)
- Mechanics: The attacker deposits 34 WBNB into the BDEX pair and manually swaps out BDEX, then calls the vault strategy's `convertDustToEarned()` which harvests against the skewed reserves, and sells the BDEX back for more WBNB than spent.
- Lesson: Public harvest/convert functions that read live pool reserves can be triggered at attacker-chosen skew moments.

## Brahma (BrahmaTOPGE)
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / Ethereum / arbitrary external call (zapIn swapTarget)
- Mechanics: `Zapper.zapIn` executes caller-supplied `swapTarget`/`callData`; the attacker sets swapTarget = USDC and callData = `transferFrom(victim → attacker)` for a victim who had approved the zapper, draining their USDC.
- Lesson: Zap-in functions must whitelist swap targets — arbitrary callData on user-approved spenders is an allowance drain.

## DFX Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / Ethereum / flash-accounting (deposit during flash callback)
- Mechanics: The attacker calls `dfx.flash()` with 99.5% of the pool's XIDR/USDC and, inside `flashCallback`, calls `dfx.deposit(200k)` while the flash accounting is in-flight — the deposit is priced against pre-flash state, minting LP too cheaply; after the flash they `withdraw` the LP and sell the proceeds.
- Lesson: Flash-loan callbacks that permit deposit/withdraw re-entry against in-flight state are exploitable — lock state during flash.

## Kashi (Abracadabra)
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / Ethereum / logic (self-liquidation + exchange-rate update)
- Mechanics: Flashloan 450k xSUSHI from BentoBox, deposit it as Cauldron collateral, borrow 800k MIM, call `updateExchangeRate()` to reprice, then liquidate their own position — taking the collateral while leaving bad debt — and repay the flashloan with profit.
- Lesson: Self-liquidation combined with in-tx exchange-rate updates can walk off with collateral — check for bad-debt invariants on liquidation.

## MBC & ZZSH
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / price manipulation via auto-liquify + donation
- Mechanics: Flashloan USDT, buy MBC, call `MBC.swapAndLiquifyStepv1()` (the token's auto-tax-swap) to skew the pair reserves, then donate 1001 USDT + all MBC to the pair (bypassing the `_isAddLiquidityV1` gate) and sell MBC at the inflated price; the same sequence is repeated on ZZSH.
- Lesson: Public auto-liquify functions plus donation gates are a repeatable reserve-manipulation combo — audit both together.

## MEV Bot 0x0ad8
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / Ethereum / access-control (bot executes attacker calldata)
- Mechanics: Calling the bot's function `0x090f88ca` with attacker-crafted calldata makes it execute `transferFrom(victim → attacker)` for a victim's full USDC balance, using the bot's pre-existing allowances.
- Lesson: MEV bots with standing approvals and unauthenticated entry points are free transferFrom machines — validate callers.

## Beefy mooCAKE+CTX
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / reward/strategy logic + flashloan
- Mechanics: The attacker pre-funds the SmartChef with CTK, flashloans 400k BUSD, mints vBUSD as Venus collateral, borrows 50k CAKE, deposits into the Beefy vault, triggers the strategy `harvest()` (via a fresh contract) to realize inflated rewards, withdraws and repays — profiting from the harvest-induced rebalancing.
- Lesson: Strategy harvest functions callable by anyone during a flashloan let attackers time reward realization — restrict harvest to the keeper.

## Numbers Protocol
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / Ethereum / bridge (permit misuse)
- Mechanics: Multichain's `anySwapOutUnderlyingWithPermit` accepts `(from, to, v, r, s)`; the attacker passes a victim as `from`, dummy signature values, and themselves as `to` — the router pulls the victim's NUM via their pre-approved allowance and "bridges" it to the attacker.
- Lesson: Bridge permit functions must verify `from == msg.sender` or validate the signature — never trust caller-supplied from/sig tuples.

## Polynomial
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / Optimism / arbitrary external call (swapTarget in zap)
- Mechanics: `swapAndDeposit(user=victim, token=ETH, depositToken=attacker, swapTarget=USDC, swapData=transferFrom(victim→attacker))` — the zap executes the attacker's swapData against the attacker-chosen target, draining five victims' USDC allowances.
- Lesson: Zap functions must whitelist swap targets and ignore attacker-controlled calldata destinations.

## SDAO
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / reward accounting manipulation
- Mechanics: Flashloan 500 USDT, buy SDAO, add LP and stake half; transferring SDAO to the pair raises `totalStakeReward` above `lastTotalStakeReward`, and `withdrawTeam(pair)` further skews the accounting — so `getReward()` pays out inflated rewards that the attacker dumps for USDT.
- Lesson: Reward accumulators driven by pair transfers (totalStakeReward) are manipulable by donations — snapshot rewards at deposit time.

## SEAMAN
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / tokenomics (dividend-on-transfer)
- Mechanics: Flashloan 800k USDT, buy SEAMAN and GVC; the attacker loops 20 transfers of 1 SEAMAN to the pair, and each transfer triggers SEAMAN's fee logic which pays dividends in GVC to holders — the attacker then sells the farmed GVC for USDT and repays.
- Lesson: Dividend-on-transfer tokens pay out during attacker-triggered transfers — model transfer fees as a farming loop.

## Sheep Farm
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / logic (referral spam)
- Mechanics: The attacker calls `register(neighbor)` 200 times in a loop, adds gems, upgrades villages, sells the village, and withdraws 20,000 wool — each register call credits team/referral rewards to the attacker without any anti-spam bound.
- Lesson: Referral registration must be idempotent or bounded — unbounded register loops farm team rewards.

## Sheep Farm
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / logic (referral spam via fresh contracts)
- Mechanics: Same Sheep Farm bug exploited with 4 fresh contracts that each register the neighbor 402 times, add gems, upgrade, sell and withdraw 156,000 wool, then selfdestruct the funds back to the attacker.
- Lesson: Same lesson as SheepFarm_exp.sol — fresh contracts multiply the referral spam until limits are enforced.

## UEarn Pool
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-11 / BSC / referral/reward logic (tier farming) + flashloan
- Mechanics: 22 fresh contracts are bound in a referral chain; the attacker flashloans 2.42M USDT and the last contract stakes 2.4M USDT to reach the top team tier, then each contract stakes 20k and claims the tiered team reward (up to 162k USDT total per level), withdrawing to the attacker and repaying the loan.
- Lesson: Tiered referral rewards are farmable with flashloaned stake and fresh-address chains — require locked, time-bound team amounts.

## AES
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-12 / BSC / fee-on-transfer + skim manipulation
- Mechanics: Flashloan 100k USDT, buy AES, donate half to the pair, then `skim(pair)` 37 times to trigger AES's transfer-fee side effects on the pair balance, call `distributeFee()`, sync, and sell AES back at the inflated price.
- Lesson: Skim loops + fee-distribution functions on fee-on-transfer tokens are a repeatable manipulation recipe.

## APC
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-12 / BSC / oracle manipulation (transSwap off live pair)
- Mechanics: Flashloan 500k USDT, pump the APC price on PancakeSwap, then `transSwap.swap(APC → MUSD)` prices the swap off the pumped pair and hands out excess MUSD; the attacker dumps APC, swaps MUSD back to APC at normal price and sells again.
- Lesson: In-house swap contracts reading live pair reserves are arbitrage engines for flashloan pump-and-dump sequences.

## BBOX
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-12 / BSC / tokenomics (sell-limit bypass via helper contract)
- Mechanics: Flashloan WBNB, buy BBOX through a fresh `TransferBBOXHelp` contract to dodge the token's sell-time limits, have the helper transfer the BBOX back, then sell 90% for WBNB — profiting from the fee/limit asymmetry.
- Lesson: Sell limits keyed per address are bypassed with fresh contracts — enforce limits at the pair/tokenomics level.

## BGLD
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-12 / BSC / migration pricing + skim manipulation
- Mechanics: Flashloan WBNB; the attacker donates to and skims the oldBGLD pair to pump its price, calls `Proxy.migrate()` converting oldBGLD→newBGLD at the manipulated rate, flashloans DEBT to repeat the skim trick on the oldBGLD/DEBT pair, and finally sells newBGLD/DEBT into USDT.
- Lesson: Token migration functions must not use manipulable spot prices — snapshots or TWAPs only.

## DFS
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-12 / BSC / fee-on-transfer + skim loop
- Mechanics: Flash-swap the pair's USDT, then flash-swap half the DFS reserve; donate DFS back (98% then 95%) and loop 12 `skim(pair)` calls plus a final `skim(attacker)`, each triggering DFS's fee-on-transfer on the pair, then swap USDT out at the skewed price and repay with 0.5% fee.
- Lesson: Fee-on-transfer + skim loops skew reserves in the attacker's favor — audit fee logic for per-transfer burns.

## Defrost Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-12 / Avalanche / share-price manipulation (flashloan deposit)
- Mechanics: Flash-swap USDC, then take the LSWUSDC vault's own flashloan and `deposit` the borrowed USDC straight back into the vault — minting shares while the vault balance is flashloan-inflated — then `redeem` those shares at the pumped rate and repay.
- Lesson: Vault share minting during a flashloan inflates the exchange rate — block deposit/redeem inside flash callbacks.

## ElasticSwap
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-12 / Avalanche / math (decay/accounting between internal and actual balances)
- Mechanics: Flash-swap TIC; the attacker repeatedly calls `addLiquidity(1e9, 0)` with tiny amounts and `removeLiquidity`, exploiting the gap between the AMM's `internalBalances` and actual token balances (donation/decay accounting) to withdraw more than deposited, plus a `swapQuoteTokenForBaseToken` with a 100× reserve amount — draining ~$850k.
- Lesson: Custom AMMs must enforce x*y=k against actual balances after every op — internal-vs-actual balance drift is a drain.

## FPR

## JayPeggers
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-12 / Ethereum / reentrancy (buyJay token callback)
- Mechanics: `buyJay` accepts an attacker-supplied NFT address and calls `transferFrom` on it during purchase; the attacker's `transferFrom` re-enters `JAY.sell()` while the buy accounting is incomplete, repeatedly selling shares at an inflated balance, and they keep the ETH difference (~15.32 ETH).

## Lodestar
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-12 / Arbitrum / oracle manipulation (donate plvGLP) + compounding borrow
- Mechanics: With flashloans from Aave/Radiant/UniswapV3, the attacker mints GLP via GMX, `donate()`s sGLP to the plvGLP depositor to pump the plvGLP exchange rate, then loops 16× `borrow(lplvGLP)` → `mint(lplvGLP)` to compound against the inflated collateral price, finally borrowing out every Lodestar market (~4500 ETH).
- Lesson: Exchange rates updated by donations + uncapped borrow-mint loops = collateral amplification — cap borrows and use TWAP pricing.

## Overnight

## RFB

## TIFI Finance
