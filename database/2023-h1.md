# Database — 2023-h1

## BEVO
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: BNB / Class: reflection-token manipulation (flashloan-assisted)
- Mechanics: BEVO is a reflective token whose deliver() burns the caller's reflected balance, inflating every other holder's token balance including the LP pair's. Attacker flash-borrows WBNB, swaps it for BEVO, calls deliver() twice and skim() on the BEVO-WBNB pair, then swaps the pair's excess BEVO for WBNB and repays the loan (144 BNB profit). The pair's reserves never matched its real balance, so the k-invariant check was satisfied by stale accounting.
- Lesson: Reflective/rebasing tokens whose deliver()/skim() can desync an LP pair's real balance from its reserves are an instant drain pattern — hunt for deliver()+skim() pairs.

## BRA
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: BNB / Class: business-logic flaw (fee/tax accounting without sync)
- Mechanics: BRA's _transfer() charges a double tax when sender or recipient is the LP pair but never calls sync(), so the pair's reserves stay larger than its actual balance. The attacker flash-loans WBNB, buys BRA, transfers it all to the pair, and repeatedly calls skim() (101x) to harvest the tax-inflated excess, then sells BRA back to USDT/WBNB.
- Lesson: Any custom token fee/tax logic that mutates balances on LP-pair interactions without a following sync() leaves the excess skimmable — check tax tokens for missing sync().

## GDS
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: BNB / Class: business-logic flaw (reward claim farming)
- Mechanics: GDS's ClaimReward flow let a deposit of LP tokens trigger claim logic keyed per contract with no lock or eligibility check. The attacker adds liquidity with flash-loaned USDT, splits LP+GDS across 100 freshly deployed helper contracts, and each helper's transferToken()/withdraw() burns a dust GDS amount and forwards the LP balance (plus swap proceeds) back to the attacker, then removes liquidity and repays.
- Lesson: Reward/vault logic that credits on deposit and can be split across unlimited fresh accounts with no time-weighting or lock is farmable — check that rewards are accrued per-user over time, not per-contract on demand.

## Midas Capital
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: Polygon / Class: oracle manipulation via read-only reentrancy
- Mechanics: Attacker deposits stMATIC as collateral and mints the WMATIC-STMATIC cToken, then triggers Curve's remove_liquidity on the MATIC/stMATIC pool, which re-enters via the ETH receive() callback while the pool (used by the oracle for the cToken price) is mid-update. During the callback the collateral price is inflated, so the attacker borrows all four Jarvis fx tokens (FJCHF/FJEUR/FJGBP/FAGEUR), then liquidates their own position and redeems the stMATIC.
- Lesson: Pricing collateral from a live pool that can be distorted mid-call via a token/ETH callback is a read-only-reentrancy oracle attack — verify oracle sources are reentrancy-safe or use TWAP.

## OmniEstate
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: BNB / Class: business-logic flaw (staking reward calculation)
- Mechanics: The staking pool's invest() accepted arbitrary parameters — the attacker invests with qty_ort=1 and end_date=0, then immediately calls withdrawAndClaim() on the created lock. The reward/lock math with an uninitialized end date granted a huge ORT payout relative to the 1-token stake, which the attacker swaps back to WBNB.
- Lesson: Audit reward and lock-time math for uninitialized or zero parameters (end_date=0, stake=1) that can grant outsized rewards.

## QTN
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: reflection-token manipulation (skim)
- Mechanics: QTN charges a buy-time limit and taxes transfers to the LP pair, crediting the pair's balance without updating reserves. After buying QTN, the attacker transfers QTN to the pair 40 times and skims the tax-inflated excess into 40 helper contracts, waits out the _timeLimitFromLastBuy, then pulls the tokens back and swaps for WETH.
- Lesson: Same family as BEVO/BRA: tokens that credit LP-pair balances via tax/reflection without sync() are skimmable; check custom _transfer logic for pair-address branches.

## Roe Finance
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: logic flaw in borrow accounting (credit delegation + LP collateral)
- Mechanics: Using a victim's credit delegation (approveDelegation), the attacker deposits USDC and borrows the protocol's WBTC/USDC LP token, then loops deposit→borrow 49 times to stack the LP position without the health check re-validating capacity. The attacker then burns the LP on Uniswap to extract both WBTC and USDC, syncs the pair, and borrows USDC to repay the Balancer flash loan.
- Lesson: Borrow capacity/health must be re-checked on every deposit/borrow iteration, especially when the collateral itself is an LP token that can be burned for both sides.

## SHOCO
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: reflection-token manipulation (deliver)
- Mechanics: SHOCO's deliver() burns the caller's share of reflected supply, raising the pair's token balance above its recorded reserves. The attacker buys SHOCO with WETH, delivers 99.999% of the balance to shrink supply, then swaps the pair's now-excess SHOCO back for more WETH than the purchase cost.
- Lesson: Reflection accounting (rTotal/rOwned rate) that lets a caller shrink supply while the LP pair holds rOwned shares is exploitable — verify deliver() cannot be called to game the pair.

## Thoreum Finance
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: BNB / Class: flawed transfer logic (self-transfer inflation)
- Mechanics: Thoreum's transfer implementation mis-accounted self-transfers: sending your full balance to yourself 15 times repeatedly grew the balance (each self-transfer credited fees/reflections on top of the amount). The attacker then swaps the inflated Thoreum for WBNB in chunks.
- Lesson: Test custom ERC20s for self-transfer (from == to) behavior — a transfer that credits the recipient without debiting the sender doubles balances.

## TINU (Tom Inu)
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: reflection-token manipulation (deliver + skim)
- Mechanics: Flash-borrowed WETH is swapped to TINU (charging the pair fees), then the attacker calls deliver() on their whole balance, which inflates the pair's TINU balance relative to reserves; skim() harvests the excess; deliver+swap is repeated, and finally the pair's WETH side is drained via a direct pair swap.
- Lesson: Canonical deliver()+skim() reflection exploit — when auditing reflective tokens, check whether pair accounting can be driven apart and harvested in one transaction.

## UFDAO
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: BNB / Class: business-logic flaw (buy/burn economics)
- Mechanics: The attacker buys the public offer of UF with USDC, then calls UFT.burn(amount, tokens, adapters, pools) which returns the underlying USDC (and more, due to the reward/fee accounting), and repeats buyPublicOffer→burn with a second size. The buy fee vs. burn payout asymmetry let the attacker walk away with more USDC than spent.
- Lesson: Audit buy→burn/claim loops for economic asymmetry — if the sum of payouts exceeds the input after fees, the loop is profitable and will be farmed.

## Upswing
- Date: 2023-01 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: custom pricing-logic manipulation
- Mechanics: UPS prices trades via a custom "pressure" state that changes on every transfer. The attacker buys UPS, then transfers their whole balance to the LP pair and skims it back 8 times, pumping their own pressure counter (fake volume), then sells the same balance back for more WETH than it cost.
- Lesson: Tokens whose price/pressure state is driven by raw transfer events rather than reserves can be gamed with self-made transfer volume — check for off-reserve state influencing swaps.

## EFVault
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: access-control / validation flaw (unchecked redeem)
- Mechanics: ENF.redeem(shares, receiver) let any caller burn an arbitrary share amount and route the underlying USDC to any receiver, without checking the caller's share balance. The PoC holds a single 1e18 ENF and redeems 676,562 shares straight to the attacker, draining the vault's USDC.
- Lesson: Redeem/withdraw paths must validate the caller's share balance against the amount — a public unauthenticated redeem is a full drain.

## Sperax USDs
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: Arbitrum / Class: flawed rebase/balance-cache logic (infinite mint via recalculation)
- Mechanics: USDs caches each address's balance and recalculates on first transfer from an address. The attacker mints 11 USDs, sends them to a not-yet-deployed address, then deploys code there (Gnosis Safe) and triggers a 1-wei transfer back — the balance-recalculation path for the fresh address credited the wrong amount, effectively duplicating the balance.
- Lesson: Rebase/balance-cache tokens must handle first-transfer-from-new-address edge cases; test the recalc path against addresses that received tokens before deployment.

## CowSwap (SwapGuard)
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: access-control / validation flaw (guard bypass)
- Mechanics: SwapGuard.envelope() was meant to enforce solvency by checking token prices and balance changes, but it trusted caller-supplied prices (0) and an allowedLoss of max. The attacker encoded DAI.transferFrom(GPv2Settlement → attacker) as an "interaction" and the guard executed it with the settlement contract's allowance, draining its DAI.
- Lesson: Solvency guards must derive prices independently (not from caller input) and must never execute arbitrary calldata with third-party allowances.

## Sheep
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: BNB / Class: deflationary-token manipulation (burn + sync)
- Mechanics: SHEEP is a deflationary token with a public burn(). After a flash loan and a WBNB→SHEEP swap, the attacker repeatedly burns their whole SHEEP balance while the pair still holds SHEEP, then calls sync() so reserves match the shrunken supply picture, and swaps the pair's SHEEP for WBNB at a favorable rate before repaying.
- Lesson: Public burn() on deflationary tokens is a liquidity-drain primitive: burning shrinks supply and re-prices the pair in the attacker's favor — check for burn + sync combinations.

## Starlink
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: BNB / Class: reflection/tax-token manipulation (transfer + skim + sync)
- Mechanics: With flash loans from three DODO pools, the attacker swaps WBNB to Starlink directly on the pair (taking 51% of the pair's Starlink), then loops: transfer the pair's whole Starlink balance back to the pair (taxing it), skim() the excess, sync() — repeating until the pair is drained, then sells Starlink for WBNB.
- Lesson: Transfer-to-pair + skim + sync loops are the fingerprint of tax/reflection token drains; flag any protocol listing such tokens or holding their liquidity.

## Dexible
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: access-control / allowance abuse (arbitrary router calldata)
- Mechanics: Dexible.selfSwap() executed attacker-supplied "router" calldata with the contract's own privileges. The attacker set spender=Dexible and routerData=TRU.transferFrom(victim → attacker); because victims had approved Dexible, the swap contract's execution of that calldata moved the victims' TRU to the attacker (a 15 USDC fee paid for the privilege).
- Lesson: Aggregators that execute arbitrary user-supplied swap data while holding user approvals are allowance-theft vectors — the router/spender/calldata trio must be constrained.

## SwapX
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: BNB / Class: access-control / allowance abuse (unvalidated from-address)
- Mechanics: The unverified SwapX aggregator's swap function (selector 0x4f1f05bc) let the caller pass an arbitrary victim address as the funds source. For each of 16 victims who had approved SwapX, the attacker routed BUSD→WBNB→DND swaps charged to the victim and received the DND, then sold the DND for WBNB.
- Lesson: Swap aggregators must hard-code msg.sender as the funds source — a caller-chosen "from" turns every existing approval into free money.

## Revert Finance
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: access-control / allowance abuse (lying token + arbitrary swapData)
- Mechanics: V3Utils.swap() accepted an arbitrary swapData executed against the tokenOut. The attacker passed a fake ERC20 whose transferFrom returns true without moving funds and whose balanceOf lies (1 then 0), and swapData = USDC.transferFrom(victim → attacker). The victim had approved V3Utils, so the contract's own allowance was spent to pay the swap while the fake-token balance check passed.
- Lesson: If a swap utility lets callers supply both the swap target and the token, attackers will pair lying tokens with real approvals — validate tokens and swap targets against whitelists.

## FDP
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: BNB / Class: reflection-token manipulation (deliver)
- Mechanics: After a DODO flash loan and a WBNB→FDP swap, the attacker calls FDP.deliver() on a huge amount, which redistributes reflected supply so the pair's real FDP balance exceeds its reserve accounting. A direct pair swap then drains nearly all the pair's WBNB before repaying the loan.
- Lesson: deliver()-based reflection tokens + LP pairs = drain; the pair must be excluded from reflection redistribution or the token excluded from pools.

## DYNA
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: BNB / Class: business-logic flaw (staking reward farm)
- Mechanics: The staking contract paid rewards on deposit regardless of staking duration. The attacker seeds 200 helper contracts with deposits, then flash-borrows the pair's DYNA and loops: send DYNA to a helper, deposit, immediately redeem — each helper's withdraw() forwards its whole DYNA balance to the attacker, farming the per-deposit reward across all helpers before repaying the pair.
- Lesson: Rewards must accrue per-user per-second, and flash-loaned/instant deposits must never qualify — check whether deposit→redeem in one block yields profit.

## Platypus Finance
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: Avalanche / Class: logic flaw / insolvency (emergency path skips solvency check)
- Mechanics: The attacker deposits USDC, stakes the LP-USDC in MasterPlatypus, borrows USP up to the borrow limit, then calls emergencyWithdraw() which removes the staked collateral WITHOUT repaying or re-checking the outstanding debt. The freed USDC is withdrawn and the borrowed USP is swapped to USDC/USDT/BUSD/DAI-E — a classic collateral-with-debt-out flow.
- Lesson: Every withdrawal path — including emergency/escape-hatch functions — must re-check solvency; emergencyWithdraw that ignores debt is a free borrow.

## LaunchZone
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: BNB / Class: access-control / allowance abuse (unverified aggregator)
- Mechanics: Same SwapX-style hole: the unverified swapX implementation executed a payload that swept LZ tokens from the deployer via the deployer's standing approval, crashing LZ's price on Biswap. The attacker then bought LZ with 50 BUSD on the crashed pool and sold on Pancake for ~88.9k BUSD.
- Lesson: Unverified aggregator contracts holding user allowances are prime targets — the exploit is "call with victim as from, dump the tokens, arbitrage the resulting price gap".

## Orion Protocol
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: Ethereum + BNB / Class: reentrancy (CEI violation in swap/deposit)
- Mechanics: The attacker lists an attacker-controlled token (ATK) whose transfer() re-enters Orion's depositAsset() whenever the attacker holds USDT. During swapThroughOrionPool(USDC→ATK→USDT), the pool's internal USDT accounting is updated after the external ATK transfer, so the reentrant deposit double-credits USDT, which the attacker withdraws and swaps to WETH.
- Lesson: Swap/deposit functions that touch attacker-controlled tokens must follow checks-effects-interactions — any external call before state update is a reentrancy window.

## dForce
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: Arbitrum / Class: oracle manipulation via read-only reentrancy (Curve)
- Mechanics: The attacker deposits wstETHCRV-gauge as collateral and borrows USX, then calls Curve's remove_liquidity on the wstETH/wstETHCRV pool. Curve's ETH callback fires while the pool's balances are mid-update, and in that window the price of the vWSTETHCRVGAUGE collateral (priced from the Curve pool) collapses, letting the attacker liquidate their own and a victim's borrow positions at the distorted price and redeem the gauge collateral.
- Lesson: Read-only reentrancy through Curve/Balancer callbacks while a lending market reads the manipulated pool price is a repeatable pattern (dForce, Midas, Sentiment, Sturdy) — check oracle reads inside token/ETH callbacks.

## BonqDAO
- Date: 2023-02 / Chain: Polygon / Class: oracle manipulation (TellorFlex spot submission)
- Mechanics: The TellorFlex oracle for wALBT accepted fresh submissions with only a 10 TRB stake and no dispute window. Tx1: the attacker reports wALBT at an astronomically high price, opens a trove with 0.1 wALBT, and borrows 100M BEUR. Tx2: they report the price near zero and liquidate every other wALBT trove, seizing their collateral, then repay their own trove.
- Lesson: Oracles that let a staker submit an arbitrary price with a stake smaller than the borrowable profit are exploitable in both directions (mint high, liquidate low) — require TWAP/VWAP or a dispute window via getDataBefore.

## BIGFI
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: BNB / Class: deflationary-token manipulation (burn)
- Mechanics: The attacker flash-loans USDT, swaps to BIGFI, and computes the exact burn amount so the pair's BIGFI balance drops to ~1 while reserves stay recorded. After burning and calling sync(), the attacker swaps their BIGFI back to USDT at the old reserve ratio and repays the loan.
- Lesson: Burn/supply-reduction functions on tokens with LP pairs let attackers shrink the pair's redeemable balance while reserves remain — check burn arithmetic against pair reserves.

## DKP
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: BNB / Class: oracle manipulation (pair-balance-ratio price)
- Mechanics: The closed-source DKP exchange priced USDT→DKP using the live ratio of the USDT-DKP pair balances. The attacker flash-borrows the pair's USDT (crashing the ratio), and in the callback a fresh contract exchanges 100 USDT for a massive amount of DKP at the distorted rate, then sells DKP back through Pancake for profit.
- Lesson: Spot-pair-balance ratios as exchange prices are one flash loan away from free money — require TWAP or a proper oracle.

## Thena
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: BNB / Class: logic flaw / missing validation (reward pool unstake)
- Mechanics: ThenaRewardPool.unstake() accepted arbitrary token/pool parameters without verifying the caller had actually staked. The attacker's helper calls unstake(BUSD, 0, attackerPool, true) and immediately forwards the BUSD the reward pool pays out — claiming rewards for a non-existent staking position.
- Lesson: Reward pools must verify a real staked position for the (token, pool, user) tuple before paying; arbitrary params in claim/unstake are free-reward bugs.

## Phoenix
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: Polygon / Class: access-control (unprotected delegatecall)
- Mechanics: PhxProxy.delegateCallSwap() executed arbitrary calldata via delegatecall with no access control. The attacker first calls buyLeverage() (which leaves WETH inside the proxy), then passes a Router swap that trades the proxy's own WETH balance into the attacker's token — because the delegatecall runs in the proxy's storage/context, the attacker spends the proxy's funds.
- Lesson: Never expose a delegatecall with caller-supplied data; if one exists, it spends the contract's own balances — this is an instant drain.

## Euler Finance
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: logic flaw (liquidation accounting / donateToReserves)
- Mechanics: Using eDAI as self-referential collateral, the attacker mints 200M eDAI and 200M dDAI debt, repays 10M, mints again, then donates 100M eDAI to reserves — an operation the health-check math did not account for. The donation made the violator account liquidatable, and the attacker liquidates their own position capturing the liquidation yield, then withdraws the remaining DAI (38.9M).
- Lesson: Health-factor and liquidation math must be invariant under mint/donate/repay sequences — audit token-accounting edge cases (donations, self-collateral) that break the collateral≥debt invariant.

## SafeMoon
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: BNB / Class: access-control (public mint/burn)
- Mechanics: Two separate bugs in one contract: mint() was callable by anyone, so the attacker minted the bridge-burn address's entire balance of SFM; separately burn() was public, so the attacker burned the LP pair's SFM and the token's own balance, then synced and swapped the pair's WBNB out (27.4k WBNB).
- Lesson: mint/burn with public visibility is an infinite-mint/drain primitive — every mint/burn must be owner- or module-gated, and pair addresses must be excluded.

## DBW
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: BNB / Class: business-logic flaw (dividend accounting)
- Mechanics: DBW's static-income dividends are based on the percentage of LP a user holds at claim time, ignoring transfers and duration. The attacker flash-mints LP by donating USDT to the pair and calling Pair.mint, splits the LP across 18 mini-proxy clones, and each clone pledges the LP, claims getStaticIncome(), and redeems — farming dividends repeatedly per account before repaying.
- Lesson: Dividend systems must be time-weighted and per-deposit tracked; "share of LP at claim time" rewards can be farmed with flash-minted LP and account splitting.

## ParaSpace
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: logic flaw (circular collateral / missing health recheck)
- Mechanics: The attacker flash-loans wstETH, supplies it, and borrows cAPE; slave contracts then re-supply the borrowed cAPE as fresh collateral and borrow more cAPE, looping 8x to stack collateral on top of borrowed funds without the protocol re-validating solvency between steps. The final APE deposit into APE staking and 3 borrows (wstETH/USDC/WETH) leave the protocol under-collateralized.
- Lesson: Borrowed assets must never be immediately re-suppliable as their own collateral; add timelocks or per-step health checks to break circular collateral loops.

## ParaSpace — Paraspace_exp_2.sol
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: logic flaw (circular collateral / missing health recheck)
- Mechanics: A cleaner reconstruction of the same attack: Aave flash loan of 47.35k wstETH, then 8 slave contracts each supply wstETH and borrow cAPE (the borrowed cAPE is re-supplied by the attacker as more collateral each round), APE is deposited into APE staking to pump the borrowable base, and finally wstETH/USDC/WETH are borrowed and swapped to repay the flash loan. ParaSpace's fix was a withdrawal-and-borrow timelock.
- Lesson: The same circular-collateral loop is visible twice in the PoC folder — when a protocol lets you borrow X against Y and instantly re-supply X as Y's collateral, it is leverage without limit.

## Poolz Finance
- Date: 2023-03 / Chain: BNB / Class: integer overflow (pool-creation amount math)
- Mechanics: CreateMassPools accepted a StartAmount of type(uint256).max - poolBalance + 2, which overflowed in the pool's accounting so the recorded pool amount wrapped while the protocol's real balance stayed intact. WithdrawToken() then paid out the entire pool balance — repeated for MNZ, SIP, WOD and ECIO, each funded by a 1 BNB purchase before selling back.
- Lesson: Pool-creation/withdraw amount math must be overflow-checked (Solidity ≥0.8 or SafeMath) — a wrap in "amount owed" is a whole-balance withdrawal.

## 0vix
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: Polygon / Class: oracle manipulation (donation-inflated vGHST price)
- Mechanics: 0vix's vGHST oracle price comes from vGHST.convertVGHST(), which reflects the GHST/vGHST backing ratio. After building a leveraged USDC position, the attacker donates 1.656M GHST straight to the vGHST contract, tripling the share price, then liquidates the leveraged position and redeems USDC collateral at the inflated valuation.
- Lesson: Share-price oracles derived from a token's own backing ratio (convertToAssets style) are manipulable by direct donation — treat balance-derived prices as attacker-controlled.

## Allbridge
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: BNB / Class: business-logic flaw (bridge pool rate imbalance)
- Mechanics: The bridge's BUSD/USDT pools keep a vUsd balance vs. token balance used for swap pricing. The attacker flash-loans 7.5M BUSD, deposits 5M into the BUSD pool and 2M into the USDT pool, then bridge-swaps USDT→BUSD (breaking the pools' internal ratio), withdraws LP to amplify the imbalance, and finally swaps 40k BUSD for ~790k USDT at the corrupted rate.
- Lesson: Bridge liquidity pools whose internal vUsd/token rate can be driven apart by a deposit→swap→withdraw sequence pay out at a corrupt rate — check pool rebalancing invariants.

## Allbridge — Allbridge_exp2.sol
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: BNB / Class: business-logic flaw (bridge pool rate imbalance)
- Mechanics: An alternate PoC of the same incident that logs the corrupted rate explicitly: flash-borrow 7.5M BUSD, swap 2M+496.7k BUSD→USDT, deposit 5M BUSD and 2M USDT into the bridge pools, bridge-swap all USDT to BUSD, then withdraw 4.83B LP from the BUSD pool — after which the pool's vUsd/tokenBalance ratio shows ~3.2 vUSD per BUSD — so a 40k BUSD bridge-swap extracts ~790k USDT.
- Lesson: The second PoC makes the invariant visible: print the pool's vUsdBalance/tokenBalance ratio before and after each step when auditing bridge pools — an imbalanced ratio is the exploit state.

## Axioma
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: BNB / Class: business-logic flaw (presale caps/vesting)
- Mechanics: Axioma's presale buyToken() had no per-address cap or vesting. The attacker flash-loans 32.5 BNB, buys AXT at the fixed presale price, and immediately sells the whole allocation on Pancake for more than the purchase cost, repaying the loan.
- Lesson: Presales need per-address caps, whitelists and vesting — an uncapped fixed-price buy that can be flipped on a DEX in the same block is free arbitrage.

## Hundred Finance (2nd hack)
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: Optimism / Class: oracle/exchange-rate manipulation (empty-market donation)
- Mechanics: The hWBTC market was nearly empty, so the attacker mints a tiny WBTC share and donates 500 WBTC to the market, inflating the exchange rate so their dust share is worth the whole market. That overvalued share is then used as collateral to liquidate borrowers in ETH/SNX/USDC/DAI/USDT/sUSD/FRAX markets, seizing their collateral for a trivial repayment.
- Lesson: Donations to near-empty cToken markets inflate exchangeRateStored and mint attacker collateral from nothing — check market TVL before trusting exchange rates in liquidation math.

## MetaPoint
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: BNB / Class: access-control (public approve on user vaults)
- Mechanics: Each MetaPoint user had a personal vault contract with a public approve() function that approved msg.sender to spend the vault's POT tokens. The attacker calls approve() on 11 victim vaults and then transferFrom() each vault's entire POT balance, swapping to USDT/WBNB.
- Lesson: Any function that grants an allowance must be owner-gated — a public approve() on vault contracts is a direct drain.

## OLIFE
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: BNB / Class: reflection-token manipulation (self-transfer + deliver)
- Mechanics: OLIFE's reflection rate = rSupply/tSupply. The attacker flash-loans WBNB, buys OLIFE, self-transfers the balance 19 times (each taxed transfer shrinks tSupply), then calls deliver() to shrink further — the pair's reflected balance rises far above its recorded reserve, so a direct pair swap returns more WBNB than the purchase cost.
- Lesson: Self-transfer loops plus deliver() are the standard way to crush a reflection token's rate — check whether transfers to self or the pair mutate the supply accounting.

## Paribus
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: Arbitrum / Class: reentrancy (CompoundV2-fork redeem)
- Mechanics: Paribus forked an old CompoundV2 with the known redeem reentrancy. After minting pETH and pUSDT collateral, the attacker calls pETH.redeem(); the ETH payout triggers receive(), where — before collateral accounting is finalized — they borrow all available USDT and WBTC. Two accounts are used to stack borrows during the reentrant window.
- Lesson: CompoundV2 forks predating the reentrancy fix are exploitable as-is — grep forks for redeem() without a reentrancy guard and for the borrow-after-redeem sequence.

## Sentiment
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: Arbitrum / Class: oracle manipulation via read-only reentrancy (Balancer)
- Mechanics: The attacker opens a Sentiment account, deposits WETH, joins the Balancer wstETH/WETH pool via the account's exec(), then exits the pool — Balancer's exit triggers a callback while the pool balances are not yet updated, and the protocol's weighted-Balancer-LP oracle reads the inflated price. Borrowing against the overpriced LP collateral yields ~1M USDC-equivalent profit.
- Lesson: Balancer join/exit callbacks let an attacker query a stale inflated pool price inside the protocol's borrow path — require oracles to be safe against read-only reentrancy (or use manipulation-resistant pricing).

## Silo Finance
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: logic error (donation + interest-rate inflation, undercollateralized borrow)
- Mechanics: With the WETH silo nearly empty, the attacker deposits dust WETH and donates 1 WETH directly, so the accruing interest rate (computed on totalDeposits that include the donation) explodes; a second account deposits LINK as collateral-only and borrows WETH. After interest accrues, the attacker borrows the entire XAI reserve — the inflated accounting lets debt exceed true collateral value.
- Lesson: Interest accrual over attacker-donated balances plus cross-asset borrow limits = undercollateralized lending; donations must not enter the interest/base-rate calculation.

## SushiSwap RouteProcessor2
- Date: 2023-04 / Chain: Ethereum / Class: validation flaw (unvalidated route + callback payer)
- Mechanics: processRoute() did not validate the pool address or the callback data. The attacker passes their own contract as the "pool"; when the router calls pool.swap(), the fake pool calls back into uniswapV3SwapCallback with data encoding (WETH, victim), and the router pays the callback by transferFrom the victim — who had approved the router — draining their WETH.
- Lesson: Routers must whitelist/validate pools and never trust callback-encoded payer addresses; a fake pool + real approval is a complete theft vector.

## Swapos
