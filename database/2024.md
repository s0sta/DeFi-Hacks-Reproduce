# Database — 2024

## Barley Finance (wBARL wrapper)
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / logic flaw (flash-callback bonding at stale rate)
- Mechanics: wBARL.flash() lends the wrapper's entire BARL reserve and, critically, allows the attacker to bond() the flashed BARL inside the callback at the pre-flash exchange rate. The PoC loops 20×: approve 10 DAI (the small flash fee), flash the whole BARL balance, and in the callback bond every BARL received back into wBARL — minting wBARL cheaper than it is worth. The attacker then debonds the whole position at 100% and swaps the BARL to WETH via Uniswap V3, netting ~$130K.
- Lesson: A token wrapper's flash function must not let the flash recipient mint/bond at the pre-flash rate inside the callback — audit flash+bond/debond cycles as a pair, not in isolation.

## Bmi Zapper (Bmi)
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / access-control (missing account==msg.sender check → arbitrary aggregator call)
- Mechanics: The zapper's zapToBMI() has no check that the `account` whose funds are moved is msg.sender, and it executes attacker-supplied `_aggregator`/`_aggregatorData` as a raw call. The PoC passes the victim as account and calldata for `USDC.transferFrom(victim, attacker, victimBalance)` as aggregator data; because the victim had approved USDC to the zapper, the zapper executes the transferFrom itself and 114,000 USDC flows to the attacker.
- Lesson: Any function that spends a user's allowance on their behalf must enforce `account == msg.sender` AND never forward raw user-supplied calldata to an arbitrary address.

## Citadel Finance (CIT staking)
- 2024-01 / Arbitrum / oracle manipulation (redemption priced off spot WETH/USDC pair)
- Mechanics: The attacker stakes 2,653 CIT at a fixed rate, then flashloans 4,500 WETH and dumps it through the Camelot WETH/USDC pair, crashing the pair price. CitadelRedeem.redeem() — flagged "flawed" in the PoC — computes the CIT redemption value from the current state of that same WETH/USDC pair, so the attacker redeems his stake for far more USDC than it is worth, swaps USDC back to WETH, and repays the flashloan, repeating the cycle.
- Lesson: Redemption/mint pricing must never be derived from a spot pair the attacker can swing with one trade — use TWAP, or cap and sanity-check the redemption against the true collateral value.

## DAO Soulmate (BUI redemption)
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / access-control (no access control on redeem)
- Mechanics: The SoulMate contract's redeem(shares, receiver) is fully permissionless. The PoC simply calls redeem with the contract's entire BUI balance as shares and the attacker as receiver, converting the protocol's own treasury-backed shares into USDC/DAI/MATIC/AAVE/ENS/ZRX/UNI paid to the attacker (~$319K). The PoC comment is literally "No access control".
- Lesson: Redemption functions that can cash out the contract's own holdings are a direct drain if not restricted — always trace who can pass arbitrary shares and who receives the proceeds.

## Freedom (FREEB)
- 2024-01 / BSC / logic flaw (buyToken ignores caller-supplied expected payment vs real price)
- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans 500 WBNB from DODO and swaps WBNB→FREE on PancakeSwap to pump the FREE/WBNB pair. He then calls FREEB.buyToken() with an attacker-chosen expectedPaymentAmount of only 5 FREE — the contract never verifies the payment against the actual pool price, so it sells the listing's tokens at the manipulated rate; the attacker sells back and repays the flashloan, keeping the spread (~74 WBNB).
- Lesson: Buy/sell functions that accept the expected payment amount as a caller parameter without checking it against the true market price are exploitable by a simple price pump.

## Gamma Strategies (Uniswap V3 LP vaults)
- 2024-01 / Arbitrum / price manipulation (flashloan-driven deposit/withdraw sequencing)
- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans 3M USDT from the WETH/USDT UniV3 pool and 2M USDC from Balancer, then runs 15 rounds of: swap in the Algebra pool to skew the USDT/USDC ratio, deposit a tiny amount into the Gamma uniproxy vault, withdraw with a price-limit swap at the manipulated rate, and re-deposit. Each deposit/withdraw cycle between the manipulated Algebra pool and the USDT/USDC UniV3 pool drains more value than it locks; the loop extracts ~$6.3M of ETH-equivalent from the vault.
- Lesson: Vault deposit/withdraw flows that let the caller supply swap price limits against a manipulable pool can be milked in a loop — cap per-call amounts and compute vault shares off TWAP rather than current pool state.

## LiquidX (LQDX) zap
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / access-control (deposit lacks account==msg.sender check; allowance spent for attacker)
- Mechanics: Per the PoC note, the zap's deposit() never checks that `account` is msg.sender, and it swaps/adds liquidity using `account`'s tokens. The attacker calls zap.deposit(victim, WETH, ...) with amounts set to the victim's full WETH allowance on the zap, so the victim's approval is spent buying LQDX and adding WETH/LQDX liquidity for the attacker.
- Lesson: Zaps and routers must spend allowances only for the caller; if a function lets you name the account whose approval is consumed, it is an allowance-drain waiting to happen.

## MIC token (swapManual / LP fee claim)
- 2024-01 / BSC / logic flaw (repeatable LP-fee claim via swapAndSendLPFee)
- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans 1,700 BUSDT, swaps into MIC and BNB, and adds MIC/WBNB liquidity. MIC.swapManual() triggers the vulnerable swapAndSendLPFee, which distributes accumulated swap fees to current LP holders without settling the claim; by transferring the LP tokens through a chain of 10 freshly-deployed helper contracts and calling swapManual() each time, the attacker collects the fee pool repeatedly, then removes liquidity and repays the flashloan (~$500K).
- Lesson: Fee-distribution functions must clear or decrement the claimable amount per LP holder — a public trigger that pays "whoever holds the LP right now" can be claimed repeatedly by rotating the LP tokens.

## Abracadabra Money (Cauldron V4 / MIM)
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / logic flaw (borrow elastic/base manipulation via repayForAll + individual repays)
- Mechanics: Inside a 300k MIM DegenBox flashloan, the attacker transfers 240k MIM to the cauldron, calls repayForAll() to clear the total borrow elastic, then repays 15 existing users' borrowParts individually — double-subtracting repays and crushing the cauldron's elastic/base ratio. With the accounting corrupted, a helper adds 100 wei of collateral and loops 90× borrow(1 wei)/repay(1 wei) (each round rounds interest accrual to zero), after which the attacker borrows the cauldron's entire MIM balance and withdraws it (~$6.5M).
- Lesson: Repay paths that let a caller repay both "everyone" and individual users without idempotent elastic accounting let an attacker crush the borrow ratio and mint/buy debt at dust prices.

## NBL Game (NFT staking)
- 2024-01 / Optimism / reentrancy (onERC721Received hook in withdrawNft)
- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans the staking contract's entire NBL balance, deposits NFT #737 and all the NBL, then calls withdrawNft(0) — which has no reentrancy protection. When the NFT is transferred back, the attacker's onERC721Received hook re-enters: it transfers the NFT back into the stake, withdraws it again and re-deposits it, duplicating the staking state. The attacker then swaps the double-counted NBL rewards to USDT/WETH and repays the flashloan (~$180K).
- Lesson: Any withdraw that sends an NFT or token to the caller before updating state is a reentrancy point — NFT staking contracts need CEI and reentrancy locks on the 721 callback path.

## Orbit Chain (Orbit Bridge)
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / bridge validation flaw (withdraw accepted forged signatures / wrong chain context)
- Mechanics: The Ethereum Orbit vault's withdraw() failed to properly validate that the validator signatures authorized this chain, vault and depositId. The PoC replays the exact attack: attacker-chosen bytes32s (sha256 of hub+vault chain data), a real Orbit-side tx hash, amount 2,308.79 WBTC, depositId 8735, and 7 fake v/r/s signatures — which the vault accepted, releasing ~$81M of WBTC/ETH/stablecoins.
- Lesson: Bridge release functions must validate every element — source chain, destination vault, depositId uniqueness, and validator threshold — one missing check on the withdrawal path is an $81M-class hole.

## Peapods Finance (ppPP wrapper)
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / logic flaw (flash-callback bonding at stale rate)
- Mechanics: Same wrapper pattern as Barley: ppPP.flash() lends the wrapper's whole PEAS reserve and lets the recipient bond() the flashed tokens inside the callback at the pre-flash rate. Looping 20× with 10 DAI per round mints ppPP cheaply; the attacker debonds the accumulated position at 100% and swaps PEAS→WETH (~$1K in this replay).
- Lesson: The same flash+bond-at-stale-rate flaw repeats across wrapper tokens — grep new wrappers for flash functions that permit mint/bond during the callback.

## Radiant Capital (new USDC market)
- 2024-01 / Arbitrum / rounding (rayDiv precision loss after liquidityIndex inflation)
- Mechanics: The attacker deposits 2M USDC into Radiant's freshly-launched USDC market, then performs 151 nested Aave-style flashloans of 2M USDC, each inflating the market's liquidityIndex; he borrows ~90.7 WETH against it. A helper contract then alternates deposit(amount)/deposit(amount<<1)/withdraw in a loop, exploiting a rounding flaw in rayDiv so each deposit/withdraw cycle siphons a little more USDC from the pool, until the pool is drained (~$4.5M).
- Lesson: Lending-market share math must be re-audited under attacker-inflated index values — a rounding loss that is harmless at normal indices becomes a siphon when the index is pushed to an extreme.

## Shell (0xa898 MEV bot)
- 2024-01 / BSC / access-control (unprotected function on MEV robot)
- Mechanics: Two "robot" contracts held SHELL/BUSD and exposed an unprotected function (selector 0x5f90d725) that moves tokens to caller-supplied addresses. The PoC loops raw calls to each robot passing the victims' full BUSD balances and the attacker as receiver until both bots are emptied, then swaps SHELL→BUSD (~1,000 BUSD).
- Lesson: MEV bots routinely leave transfer-capable functions public — a raw-call fuzz of a bot's selectors with victim/attacker arguments is a cheap bounty pattern.

## Socket / Bungee Gateway (route 406)
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / access-control (route executes arbitrary user-supplied swapExtraData)
- Mechanics: The newly-added route 406's performAction() executed the caller-supplied swapExtraData bytes without validating the caller's ownership of the funds. The PoC crafts swapExtraData = `USDC.transferFrom(victim, attacker, victim's full balance)` and calls gateway.executeRoute(406, ...); the gateway holds standing approvals from users, so the route's execution drains each victim's USDC (~$3.3M total).
- Lesson: Bridge/swap routes that execute arbitrary calldata supplied by the user must check that the user actually owns the from-token — otherwise standing approvals turn the route into a mass transferFrom.

## Wise Lending (Pendle PT pool; rounding)
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / rounding + share-price donation (incorrect health-factor check)
- Mechanics: The attacker deposits 1 wei-scale amounts and transfers pool tokens to the WiseLending contract to donate to the pool, then loops depositExactAmount(pseudoTotalPool*2-1)/withdrawExactAmount — a rounding-in-favor-of-the-protocol cycle that inflates the share price with the attacker's own donations. A second position then deposits the remaining pool tokens and borrows the maximum wstETH allowed because the health check runs against the manipulated pool state (with a mocked-fresh oracle), walking away with borrowed funds (~$464K incident).
- Lesson: Share-price donation attacks require the protocol's borrow-health check to value collateral at true market price — when collateral value comes from attacker-inflatable share accounting, borrowing caps fail.

## Wise Lending (share-price inflation, full flow)
- 2024-01 / Ethereum / rounding (2x-1 deposit donation) + bad-debt health check
- Mechanics: After seeding the pool at 2 wei underlying / 1 share, the attacker loops depositExactAmount(2x-1)/withdrawExactAmount(1) to donate collateral while minting 1 share each round, pushing the share price to 36 ETH. Six helper positions then deposit 1 share each, borrow huge amounts of wstETH/WETH/LP tokens, and withdraw 1 wei of collateral — the "incorrect health factor check" lets positions enter bad debt, and the attacker finally withdraws all donated value via his inflated shares.
- Lesson: Rounding that favors the protocol on deposit is an attacker donation channel; combined with a health check that ignores real collateral value it becomes a full bank run.

## XSIJ (BSC token)

- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans 100k BUSD from DODO and swaps it for XSIJ, pumping the token's price; then, while the Pancake pair holds >1,800 XSIJ, he transfers 1 wei of XSIJ directly to the pair in a loop, exploiting the token's fee/reflection behavior (removePoolAmount) to desynchronize the pair's real balance from its reserves. ## Babyloogn airdrop
- 2024-02 / BSC / access-control (unrestricted claim loop)
- Mechanics: The airdrop contract's claim function (selector 0xfbe81135) is callable by anyone with no per-user cap. The attacker loops the call with (1, 0) while the airdrop still holds ≥285 BABYLOOGN, draining its token balance, then swaps everything to WBNB (~2.24 WBNB).

## Blueberry Protocol (lending)
- 2024-02 / Ethereum / pricing flaw (borrow limits not enforced for newly-added markets)
- Mechanics: The whitehat supplies only 1 WETH of collateral (bWETH.mint with a Balancer-flashloaned 1 WETH) and, in the same callback, borrows ~8,616 OHM, ~913 USDC and ~0.69 WBTC, then swaps OHM→WETH and repays the flashloan. The comptroller's account-liquidity math failed to constrain borrowing in the freshly-listed markets, so ~1 WETH of collateral unlocks a six-figure loan basket ($1.4M).
- Lesson: Verify the comptroller actually prices and caps every newly-added market — a market that slips past the collateral/borrow math turns 1 unit of collateral into unlimited credit.

## Burns Defi
- 2024-02 / BSC / price manipulation (burnToHolder rewards priced off manipulable spot pair)
- Mechanics: After a 250k BUSDT DODO flashloan, the attacker pushes BUSDT through the BUSDT/WBNB pair to inject WBNB into the Burns/WBNB pair, pumping the Burns price. burnToHolder() computes the payout via getAmountsOut() on that same Burns/WBNB pair, so burning tokens pays out at the manipulated rate; the attacker repeats with two tranches, calls receiveRewards(), swaps back, and repays (~$67K).

## Compound (cUNI price feed)

- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans USDC, deposits it as cUSDC collateral, and borrows the maximum UNI the comptroller permits — computed from the UniswapAnchoredView price feed for cUNI, which mispriced UNI. ## DN404 (official token proxy)
- 2024-02 / Ethereum / access-control (unprotected init())
- Mechanics: The DN404 distribution proxy's init() has no access control and no initialized guard. ## DeezNutz (ERC-404)
- 2024-02 / Ethereum / rounding (self-transfer balance inflation in ERC-404 conversion)
- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans 2,000 WETH and swaps it all to DeezNutz, then loops 5 self-transfers; the 404 token's ERC20↔NFT conversion rounds in the sender's favor, inflating his balance each hop. ## DualPools (Venus + DualPools comptrollers)
- 2024-02 / BSC / donation/balance-accounting (exchange-rate inflation via direct token donation)
- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans BUSD and WBNB from DODO pools, supplies them to Venus as vBUSD/vWBNB and borrows 11,500 LINK; he then mints just 2 wei of DualPools dLINK and donates the 11,500 LINK directly to the dLINK contract. - Lesson: cToken exchange-rate math must use accounting-tracked totals, never raw balanceOf — a direct donation instantly inflates any lender that reads token balances for collateral value.

## EGGX (NFT claim)

- Mechanics: The attacker flashloans the EGGX/WETH UniV3 pool's entire EGGX reserve and, while holding it, calls EGGXCliam.check() for 36 staked NFT IDs — the claim function pays out based on the caller's current EGGX balance. ## "Game" auction (makeBid)
- 2024-02 / Ethereum / reentrancy (ETH refund before state update + broken minimum-bid check)
- Mechanics: newBidEtherMin() has a logic error that makes the minimum bid trivially low, and makeBid() refunds the previous bidder via an ETH transfer before settling state. ## MINER (BSC)

## Miner (Ethereum UniV3)

- Mechanics: The attacker flash-swaps 4.5M BUSD and loops 10 fresh contracts: each buys CUT, adds BUSD/CUT liquidity, sells the leftover CUT, then transfers its LP tokens to the pair contract itself and burns them — distorting the pair's LP accounting — before selling CUT again at the skewed ratio and returning the BUSD to the parent (~$1.4M).
- Lesson: LP tokens sent to the pair contract or burned change the pair's implied price — trace LP balance movement in every token flow; a burn loop is a repricing loop.

## DOGGO (balance-threshold token)
- 2024-09 / Ethereum / logic flaw (token-contract balance threshold manipulated around swap)
- Mechanics: DOGGO's transfer behavior changes around a token-contract balance threshold (~4.207e18). The attacker flashloans the UniV3 pool's DOGGO, sells a computed chunk to WETH, tops the token contract's own balance to just below the threshold, sells the remainder, and buys back the exact amount to repay — netting the spread (~$7K).
- Lesson: Tokens with mechanics keyed to their own contract balance are a price-lever — model the behavior exactly at the threshold before assuming a swap is fair.

## HANA (balance-threshold token)

## Pledge (public swapTokenU)
- 2024-12 / BSC / access-control (swapTokenU swaps the contract's own tokens to caller-chosen target)
- Mechanics: Pledge's swapTokenU(amount, target) is public: the attacker passes the contract's entire MFT balance as the amount and himself as the target, and the contract swaps its own holdings to him (~15K).
- Lesson: A swap function whose amount and target are both caller-supplied is a treasury drain — derive both from the caller's verified balance.

## SlurpyCoin (transfer-triggered buyback)
- 2024-12 / BSC / logic flaw (transfer to contract triggers BuyOrSell; 1-wei self-transfer triggers buyback)
- Mechanics: SLURPY's transfer to the token contract triggers its BuyOrSell auto-buyback, and 1-wei self-transfers trigger swapEthForTokens. The attacker flashloans 40 WBNB and loops buy/trigger cycles — each triggered buyback lifts the price — then buys via 8 helpers and sells everything back at the pumped price, repaying the flashloan (~3K).
- Lesson: Tokens with transfer-triggered buybacks give the sender a free price lever — model the token contract's own swap activity for every transfer path.
