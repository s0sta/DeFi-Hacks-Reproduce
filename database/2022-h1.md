# Database — 2022-h1

## Anyswap (Multichain)
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-01 · Ethereum · access-control / unchecked permit (signature validation bypass)
- Mechanics: anySwapOutUnderlyingWithPermit on the AnyswapV4 router took caller-supplied from/token/amount plus an unvalidated permit signature (v=0, r=s=0x0). The router called permit() on the underlying (WETH has no EIP-2612 permit, so it was a silent no-op), ignored the result, then ran safeTransferFrom(underlying, from, token, amount) against any pre-existing allowance and credited anyTokens via the token's depositVault. In the PoC the attacker's fake token self-reports underlying()=WETH, the router pulls the victim's WETH into it, and the attacker transfers it out; real-world loss ~$3M across WETH/AVAX/MATIC/WBNB pools.
- Lesson: A router that runs permit-then-transferFrom without verifying the permit actually raised the allowance lets anyone spend every stale infinite approval — compare allowance before/after the permit call.

## Qubit Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-01 · Ethereum→BSC · bridge / missing input validation (zero-address whitelist)
- Mechanics: QBridgeHandler registered the zero address for the ETH resourceID and contractWhitelist[address(0)] was true. The attacker called QBridge.deposit with data encoding (option, amount, to) for ~190 ETH worth of xETH and no ETH sent; the handler passed the zero-address "token" through the generic path, skipped any asset transfer, and only emitted a Deposit event, which the relayer trusted to mint xETH on BSC. The minted xETH was then used as collateral to drain ~$80M.
- Lesson: Sentinel addresses like 0x0 must never be whitelisted tokens, and a Deposit event must never fire without on-chain proof the asset was actually locked.

## Build Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-02 · Ethereum · governance (hostile takeover)
- Mechanics: The attacker acquired BUILD, proposed that the governor approve() their address for the DAO's entire BUILD treasury, voted it through with cheaply bought voting power, and after the timelock executed it and swept the treasury with transferFrom. (~$470K)
- Lesson: A DAO whose voting token is freely purchasable and whose treasury sits inside the governor with a generic execute() is one cheap token purchase away from takeover — voting power must be hard to buy instantly.

## Meter.io
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-02 · Moonriver · bridge / input validation (native vs wrapped token confusion)
- Mechanics: Meter's ChainBridge-fork handler trusted the amount in calldata for burn-listed wrapped assets (WETH/WBNB): calling deposit with an arbitrarily large amount and no msg.value burned nothing from the attacker yet instructed the destination to release the full amount. The PoC then swaps the freshly minted 2000 WETH for WMOVR via SushiSwap. Loss ~$4.4M.
- Lesson: In bridge handlers, wrapped/native assets must be validated against msg.value or a real transferFrom — the amount in calldata is attacker input, never a receipt.

## Sandbox
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-02 · Ethereum · access-control (public burn)
- Mechanics: The LAND NFT contract exposed its internal-style _burn(from, to, id) publicly with no authorization check, so any caller could burn any holder's LAND. The PoC burns a whale's tokenId 3738 in a loop, destroying 100 NFTs (~$500K+).
- Lesson: Grep NFT collections for public _burn/_transfer-style functions — an exposed destructor is a griefing weapon against any holder.

## TecraSpace
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-02 · Ethereum · access-control (permissionless burnFrom) + LP manipulation
- Mechanics: TCR's burnFrom was callable by anyone. The attacker bought TCR, burned nearly the entire TCR balance of its Uniswap pair, called sync() so reserves matched the depleted balance, then sold their remaining TCR back at the artificially inflated price, extracting the USDT side. Loss 639,222 USDT.
- Lesson: A token with a permissionless burnFrom lets anyone rug its own LP pool — check burn authorization before treating any burnable token as an asset.

## Agave
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Gnosis · reentrancy (ERC677 token hook in liquidation)
- Mechanics: Aave-v2 fork: the attacker built a position with health factor just above 1, warped time so accrued interest pushed it below 1, then called liquidationCall on themselves. Because the aToken transfer during liquidation triggered the Gnosis onTokenTransfer hook before liquidation state updates completed, the attacker re-entered and ran deposit/borrow loops, draining WETH and the other markets' liquidity. (~$1.5M per KeyInfo)
- Lesson: Lending-fork liquidation paths that hand control to ERC677/ERC777 token hooks mid-callback are reentrancy targets — finalize all state before any external call, liquidations included.

## Auctus
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Ethereum · access-control / arbitrary external call
- Mechanics: ACOWriter.write accepted an arbitrary acoToken, strikeAsset and data blob and executed the caller-supplied data against the strike asset with the writer's own authority. The attacker passed a fake ACO token plus a transferFrom(victim-with-standing-approval, attacker, balance) on USDC, draining funds the writer was authorized to move. (~$1M)
- Lesson: Routers that execute arbitrary calldata on user-chosen assets inherit every standing approval granted to the router — constrain data to a whitelisted action set.

## Bacon Protocol
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Ethereum · reentrancy (ERC777 hook)
- Mechanics: bacon.lend updated its exchange-rate accounting after the ERC777 HOME token transfer; the tokensReceived hook re-entered lend twice more, minting bHOME at the pre-update rate, and redeem then converted the inflated balance back to USDC, repaying the Uniswap flashswap with profit. (~$1M)
- Lesson: ERC777/ERC677 receiver hooks let borrowers re-enter before share-price updates — apply CEI and reentrancy guards to every mint/redeem pair.

## Compound (cTUSD)
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Ethereum · business logic / token migration
- Mechanics: After TUSD's legacy→new migration, the cTUSD market's cash sat in the legacy contract. sweepToken only rejected the current underlying address, so sweeping the legacy TUSD — economically 1:1 with new TUSD — moved the market's entire liquidity out; the market was drained (later patched via governance).
- Lesson: Address-equality checks for "the underlying" break across token migrations — block economically equivalent legacy tokens too, and keep sweep functions admin-gated.

## Fantasm
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Fantom · rounding / decimal-scaling error
- Mechanics: The xFTM pool's mint() mis-normalized FSM's decimals when computing _xftmOut, so depositing 100 FSM minted far more xFTM than the pool ratio justified; the attacker then called collect() to realize the inflated yield. (~$2.6M)
- Lesson: Cross-token decimal normalization in mint/redemption math is a one-line bug with nine-figure impact — unit-test share math against non-18-decimal tokens.

## Hundred Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Gnosis · reentrancy (ERC677 hook)
- Mechanics: A Compound fork whose hxdai borrow transfers xDAI through an ERC677-compatible token fired onTokenTransfer before the comptroller recorded the new borrow. The attacker flashswapped USDC, deposited, borrowed, and re-entered on the token hook to borrow more xDAI against collateral not yet accounted for, converting it to USDC via Curve.
- Lesson: Forks that substitute ERC677/ERC777 tokens into Compound-style borrow paths inherit a callback window — re-audit every external-call ordering in the fork diff.

## LiFi
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Ethereum · access-control / arbitrary external call (standing approvals)
- Mechanics: The pre-bridge swap feature let callers pass an array of arbitrary callTo + callData (any contract, any method); the contract only verified the ending balance covered the bridge amount. The attacker chained one legitimate swap with ~37 transferFrom calls on USDT/USDC/DAI/MATIC executed from the LiFi contract's address, sweeping every wallet that had granted it infinite approval. (~$600K)
- Lesson: Flexible swap adapters are arbitrary-call executors running under the contract's identity — standing approvals become a honeypot; allowlist callTo and never let user calldata reach token transferFrom.

## OneRing
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Fantom · rounding / decimal-scaling error
- Mechanics: The vault's share price used 18-decimal math for USDC (6 decimals), so depositSafe of an 80M USDC flashloan minted wildly inflated shares; withdrawing the full share balance paid out more USDC than was deposited. (~$1.45M)
- Lesson: Share-price math must derive decimals from the token, not hardcode 1e18 — a flashloan deposit/withdraw round-trip is the fastest way to find this bug.

## Paraluni
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · BSC · flashloan + untrusted-token accounting (MasterChef LP share mint)
- Mechanics: MasterChef's depositByAddLiquidity trusted the caller-supplied token pair: the attacker's EvilToken contracts returned max allowance and fake balances, and their transferFrom approved the chef for the attacker's real USDT/BUSD instead of moving anything. The chef minted LP shares for a near-zero deposit; withdrawAndRemoveLiquidity then drained the USDT/BUSD pool. (~$1.7M)
- Lesson: Farm contracts must not trust caller-supplied token contracts' allowance()/balanceOf()/transferFrom() — fake tokens mint LP shares for free.

## Redacted Cartel
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Ethereum · access-control / allowance accounting logic error
- Mechanics: wxBTRFLY's custom transferFrom called _approve(sender, msg.sender, allowance(sender, recipient) - amount) — it re-keyed the allowance from the recipient's allowance instead of msg.sender's. Bob calls transferFrom(Alice, AliceContract, 0): no funds move, but Bob receives an allowance equal to Alice's allowance to AliceContract, then sweeps Alice's full balance.
- Lesson: A hand-rolled transferFrom that reads the wrong allowance key grants spenders permissions they were never given — fuzz allowance transitions, including transferFrom with amount=0.

## Revest Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Ethereum · reentrancy
- Mechanics: withdrawFNFT transferred ERC1155 FNFTs to the caller before finalizing the FNFT value bookkeeping; via onERC1155Received the attacker re-entered depositAdditionalToFNFT on the adjacent FNFT id, inflating its per-unit value to 1e18 RENA mid-withdraw, so the 360,001 withdrawn units paid out far more RENA than was deposited. (~$2M)
- Lesson: Vesting/NFT-lock withdraws must finalize mappings before transferring the NFT — the receive hook is a free re-entry point.

## Ronin Bridge
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Ethereum/Ronin · signature validation / access-control (inverted validator check)
- Mechanics: The bridge's signature-threshold loop inverted its validator check (counting non-validator signers toward the threshold), so five signatures from any arbitrary key satisfied the "5-of-9" requirement; the attacker submitted withdrawERC20For for 173,600 ETH and 25.5M USDC with forged signatures. $625M — the largest DeFi theft to date.
- Lesson: Multisig verification loops are where a single inverted ! or misplaced increment silently deletes the whole threshold — write exhaustive signer-set permutation tests.

## TreasureDAO
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Arbitrum · missing validation (zero-quantity purchase)
- Mechanics: The marketplace's buyItem computed total price as pricePerItem × quantity and never checked quantity > 0, so a purchase with quantity=0 transferred the SmolBrain NFT for 0 MAGIC; the attacker repeated this to sweep 100+ NFTs. (~$1.4M)
- Lesson: Price-by-multiplication without a quantity>0 check turns any marketplace into a free-mint — audit every payable transfer for paths where the computed price can be zero.

## Umbrella Network
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-03 · Ethereum · arithmetic underflow (missing balance check in withdraw)
- Mechanics: StakingRewards.withdraw only required amount != 0, then executed _balances[user] -= amount (underflow) and transferred amount LP tokens to the caller. With no prior stake, the attacker withdrew the pool's entire UniLP balance. (~$700K)
- Lesson: Withdraw functions that transfer amount before (or without) checking balance >= amount are infinite-withdraw bugs regardless of the "safe math" comments above them.

## Akutar
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · Ethereum · DoS + logic error (refund loop + inverted require)
- Mechanics: Two flaws: (1) processRefunds iterated bidders pushing ETH refunds, so a contract bidder whose fallback reverts bricks the entire refund loop, trapping honest bidders' funds; (2) claimProjectFunds used an inverted require condition, permanently locking the ~$34M of project funds (11,539 ETH).
- Lesson: Refund loops must be pull-based (or skip reverting recipients), and one inverted require can lock nine figures — test failure branches, not just the happy path.

## Beanstalk
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · Ethereum · governance + flashloan
- Mechanics: Depositing BEAN3CRV LP into the Silo granted instant BIP voting power and emergencyCommit executed a proposal with >=2/3 support immediately. The attacker flashloaned ~$1B (DAI/USDC/USDT) from Aave, added Curve liquidity, deposited the LP for votes, and passed BIP-18, which donated the protocol's BEAN3CRV LP to the attacker's contract; liquidity was removed and the flashloan repaid. $181M.
- Lesson: Governance that weights votes on instantly deposit-able (flashloanable) liquidity plus immediate emergency execution is directly purchasable — votes need lock-ups and proposals need time-locks.

## Elephant Money
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · BSC · oracle manipulation (spot-price treasury) + flashloan
- Mechanics: The treasury's mint/redeem valued TRUNK off the manipulable PancakeSwap ELEPHANT price. The attacker flashloaned 100k WBNB, pumped ELEPHANT, minted 90M TRUNK against the inflated treasury value, redeemed for BUSD, then dumped ELEPHANT back and repaid. (~$11M)
- Lesson: Treasury tokens minted/redeemed at live DEX spot prices are flashloan-pumpable — value mints against TWAP or capped backing, never spot.

## Gym Network (migration)
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · BSC · flashloan + migration ratio manipulation
- Mechanics: The liquidity-migration contract converted old-pair LP to new-pair LP at a ratio derived from manipulable pool balances. The attacker flashswapped WBNB, added liquidity to the old GYM pair at an artificial ratio (using the migrator's own GYMNET holdings as the amount), migrated the LP, removed liquidity from the GYMNET pair, and swapped back for profit.
- Lesson: Token-migration ratios computed from live pool balances are manipulable in one transaction — migrations must use fixed, on-chain-verified conversion rates.

## Rari Capital (Fuse)
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · Ethereum · reentrancy (comptroller exitMarket during borrow)
- Mechanics: fETH.borrow transferred ETH before the borrow was recorded; in the receive() hook the attacker called comptroller.exitMarket on their collateral market, exploiting stale collateral/borrow accounting so the 1,977 ETH borrow passed against collateral that was then redeemed out, leaving the loan uncollateralized. (Part of the ~$80M Fuse hack)
- Lesson: Compound-style forks where borrow pays out before recording the debt are reentrancy-open — trace the receive() hook reachable during the ETH transfer against every comptroller state update.

## Rikkei Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · BSC · access-control (permissionless oracle update)
- Mechanics: SimplePriceOracle.setOracleData had no access control, so the attacker pointed the rBNB oracle at a malicious feed returning the Chainlink BNB price x1e10; a dust rBNB deposit was then valued astronomically and the attacker borrowed the market's entire USDC cash. (~$1.1M)
- Lesson: An oracle setter callable by anyone is a full bank takeover — audit every setOracleData/setPrice admin function for access control before anything else.

## Saddle Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · Ethereum · math error (metapool virtual price) + flashloan
- Mechanics: The sUSD metapool's swap math mispriced the SaddleUSDV2 LP token (virtual-price accounting), so round-tripping sUSD -> LP token -> sUSD with a 15M USDC flashloan returned more sUSD than it consumed; the attacker repeated the round trip and cashed out via Curve. (~$10M)
- Lesson: Metapool swaps involving the pool's own LP token are prone to virtual-price bugs — property-test round-trip swaps (A->LP->A must never gain) for every meta pool.

## Wdoge
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · BSC · flashloan + DEX accounting manipulation (skim/sync)
- Mechanics: The attacker flashswapped 2,900 WBNB and donated it straight to the WDoge pair, then alternated swaps, direct token donations, skim() and sync() to harvest the balance-vs-reserve drift, walking away with the pair's WBNB after repaying the flashswap.
- Lesson: Pairs with externally donatable balances and public skim/sync are accounting-manipulation targets — watch any token whose transfers into a pair do not route through swap().

## Zeed
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · BSC · flashloan + DEX accounting manipulation (skim cycles)
- Mechanics: The attacker flashswapped the USDT-YEED pair's entire YEED reserve, donated YEED directly to the pair, then cycled skim() across three linked pairs (USDT-YEED -> HO-YEED -> ZEED-YEED) to funnel the excess into their own address, repaid the flashswap and dumped the accumulated YEED for USDT. (~$1M)
- Lesson: Multi-pair skim() chains turn token donations into arbitrage — pairs sharing a token must distrust balanceOf drift caused by fee-on-transfer or reflection tokens.

## CF Token
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · BSC · access-control (public internal function)
- Mechanics: The CF token left _transfer() callable by anyone (visibility defaulting to public in the old compiler), so the attacker simply invoked _transfer(pair, attacker, 1e21) to move the pair's CF balance out and dump it (930 CF in the PoC).
- Lesson: In pre-0.5 Solidity a missing visibility modifier on an "internal" function makes it public — grep tokens for _transfer/_mint/_burn without an explicit internal keyword.

## DEUS Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-04 · Fantom · signature validation / access-control (broken MuSig verification)
- Mechanics: DeiLenderSolidex.borrow verified the MuSig-Schnorr signature against the public key embedded in the attacker-supplied report ID (repID) instead of a registered signer, so one self-made signature unlocked a ~17.2M DEI loan against LP collateral; the attacker dumped DEI for USDC and repaid the flash-minted USDC. (~$13M)
- Lesson: Multi-signature schemes that derive the signer key from attacker-controlled data validate nothing — the key must come from immutable storage and the threshold must be real.

## ApeCoin airdrop / NFTX
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-05 · Ethereum · flashloan + airdrop-claim (transient ownership)
- Mechanics: The attacker flash-borrowed BAYC vault tokens from NFTX, redeemed specific BAYC NFTs, claimed the APE airdrop for each (the airdrop only checked current ownership), then re-minted the NFTs into the vault and repaid the flashloan — 60,564 APE for zero capital.
- Lesson: Airdrops that check only the instantaneous owner are farmable by anyone who can borrow the NFT — claims must be tied to historical snapshots or non-borrowable ownership.

## Fortress Loans
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-05 · BSC · governance + oracle manipulation
- Mechanics: The attacker passed a GovernorAlpha proposal (with cheaply bought MAHA votes) raising the fFTS collateral factor to 0.7, then submitted forged price data (FTS-USD = 4e34) to the Umbrella-style Chain oracle whose submit() signature check accepted it, so ~$2 of FTS collateral was valued astronomically and all 13 lending markets were drained. (~$3M per KeyInfo)
- Lesson: Governance-set collateral factors plus a permissionless/forgable price oracle is a two-step vault key — model "attacker passes CF proposal" and "attacker submits price" as one exploit chain.

## HackDao
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-05 · BSC · flashloan + skim/sync manipulation
- Mechanics: The attacker bought HackDao with a DODO flashloan, donated it to the WBNB pair, used skim() to shuttle the excess into a second pair and back (pair-to-pair skim cycling) so the first pair's token balance exceeded its reserves, then swapped WBNB out at the manipulated ratio before repaying the flashloan.
- Lesson: Any pair whose token balance can be pushed above reserves via direct transfer plus skim is a price-oracle-free arbitrage target — the same donation/skim pattern as Zeed and Wdoge.

## Novo
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-05 · BSC · access-control (broken transferFrom) + price manipulation
- Mechanics: NOVO's transferFrom did not validate allowances, so the attacker pulled 113.9M NOVO directly out of the NOVO/WBNB pair, called sync() to collapse the pair's NOVO reserves, then sold their flash-bought NOVO back at the manipulated price and repaid the 17.2 WBNB flashswap.
- Lesson: A token whose transferFrom skips the allowance check is a portable "drain any pair" weapon — verify allowance math on every new token before touching its pools.

## Discover
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-06 · BSC · access-control (permissionless credit)
- Mechanics: ETHpledge.pledgein credited the caller-specified "father address" with DIS value for a caller-specified amount without taking the corresponding payment; the attacker used a BUSD flashswap to satisfy input requirements and walked away with credited DIS to dump (the PoC ends before the dump; the final router swap is expected to fail).
- Lesson: Pledge/referral functions that credit value to an arbitrary address must pull or burn the equivalent input — check that every credit has a matching debit in the same call.

## Gym Network (single pool)
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-06 · BSC · accounting flaw (deposit without payment)
- Mechanics: GymSinglePool.depositFromOtherContract credited the caller's shares for 8,000 GYMNET without transferring the tokens in (the amount was never pulled from the caller), and after the deposit window the attacker's withdraw paid out real GYMNET from the pool.
- Lesson: A deposit function that credits shares without a corresponding transferFrom is a free-money faucet — verify the debit exists for every credit in staking pools.

## Harmony Horizon Bridge
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-06 · Ethereum · multisig key compromise (opsec)
- Mechanics: The Horizon bridge's 2-of-5 multisig only needed two signatures and the attacker had compromised two of the five keys; the PoC replays the exact submitTransaction + confirmTransaction pair that unlocked 9.98M USDT to the attacker. $100M.
- Lesson: Smart contracts cannot save a 2-of-N multisig whose keys live in one place — hunting includes opsec: threshold size, key custody diversity, and whether confirmations are truly independent.

## Optimism (Wintermute)
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-06 · Optimism · deployment gap / account takeover (CREATE2 claim)
- Mechanics: Wintermute's multisig existed on Ethereum but was never deployed on Optimism, leaving a funded EOA; the attacker brute-forced Gnosis Safe ProxyFactory createProxy salts until CREATE2 landed at Wintermute's address, then controlled the 20M OP sitting there. The PoC loops createProxy until childcontract == 0x4f3a120E72C76c22ae802D129F599BFDbc31cb81.
- Lesson: Addresses holding funds on an L2 without deployed code are claimable by anyone via CREATE2 factory grinding — check for funded-but-codeless accounts and replay deployments before funding.

## Inverse Finance
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-06 · Ethereum · oracle manipulation (spot price via Curve pool) + flashloan
- Mechanics: The yvCurve3Crypto price feed read the Curve 3crypto pool's live price; the attacker flashloaned 27,000 WBTC, added liquidity and swapped to pump the pool's price, re-valuing their anYvCrv3Crypto collateral so they could borrow ~10.1M DOLA, then unwound via USDT/WBTC swaps and kept the margin. (~$1.26M)
- Lesson: LP-token collateral priced off a spot pool is flashloan-pumpable — LP oracles need TWAP or manipulation resistance, and single-block mega-swaps should be sanitized.

## Snood
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-06 · Ethereum · access-control (broken transferFrom) + price manipulation
- Mechanics: SNOOD's transferFrom ignored approvals, so the attacker drained the Uniswap pair's entire SNOOD balance, called sync() to shrink the reserves, returned the tokens, and swapped WETH out at the manipulated price.
- Lesson: Same pattern as Novo — a no-allowance-check transferFrom plus sync() is a two-call pair drain; verify allowance enforcement before trusting any pool with a new token.

## XCarnival
- Date/Chain/Class: 2022-06 · Ethereum · access-control / state-machine (untrusted xToken param + non-invalidated orders)
- Mechanics: pledgeAndBorrow trusted the caller-supplied xToken address; the attacker pledged BAYC #5110 with a fake xToken whose borrow() did nothing (no debt recorded), withdrew the NFT, then called the real XToken.borrow on the same orderId — withdrawNFT never invalidated the order — so each of 33 payload contracts borrowed 36 ETH against the same already-withdrawn NFT. 3,087 ETH lost (~$3.87M).
- Lesson: NFT-lending flows must invalidate orders on withdrawal and validate the xToken parameter — draw the pledge/withdraw/borrow state machine first when auditing.
