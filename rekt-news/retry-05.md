## bZx — Nov 6, 2021 — ~$55M
- Chain/Attack vector: Polygon + BSC (then Ethereum for cashing out); private-key compromise of a developer EOA via a spear-phishing email — no smart-contract exploit, then a malicious contract update to sweep user approvals.
- Root cause: A bZx developer opened a phishing email whose attached Word document ran a malicious macro, compromising the dev's personal mnemonic phrase — and that same EOA held control over bZx's Polygon and BSC deployments. Single-EOA control of protocol deployments (no multisig/hardware key for admin roles) turned one personal laptop compromise into full protocol control.
- Mechanics: With the compromised key the attacker took control of the Polygon/BSC contracts and drained BZRX, then updated the contract code to extract tokens from every wallet that had granted approvals to the affected contracts (so the attack expanded from the treasury to individual users). To avoid dumping illiquid BZRX, the attacker sent stolen BZRX to Ethereum as collateral and borrowed other assets instead. Slowmist kept a running total of ~$55M lost; bZx asked Circle to freeze stolen USDC, while USDT and funds on Binance were frozen quickly. The team published a preliminary post-mortem with the hacker's addresses across Polygon, BSC and Ethereum, and offered to "chat and reach an agreement".
- Lesson: (1) No high-TVL protocol's deployments/upgrade rights may sit behind a single dev EOA — admin keys need multisig + hardware wallets + rotation, because spear-phishing a developer is the cheapest high-yield attack in Web3; (2) after any admin-key compromise, standing user token approvals become a second wave of theft — auditors/hunters should check whether a compromised protocol can patch contracts to sweep approvals, and whether a pause/kill-switch exists that works even with admin keys lost.
- REKT narrative extras: rekt's debut entry for bzx.network went straight into the leaderboard top 10; it was bZx's 4th incident — the Feb 2020 pair ($298k and $645k) were literally the first flash-loan-based exploits, and Sep 2020's $8M was taken and later returned. Community replies ranged from exasperation ("What is this like 4th time?") to "Y'all ngmi"; rekt's closer — human error should never have been able to lead to such an enormous loss, but "it takes an especially callous and greedy hacker" to also drain individual users' wallets.
