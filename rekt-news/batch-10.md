# REKT.news knowledge cards — batch-10

## TempleDAO — 2022-10-12 — ~$2.3M in LP tokens
*source slug: temple-rekt (500) → resolved to templedao-rekt*
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; missing parameter validation in STAX's `StaxLPStaking.migrateStake()` — anyone could trigger a fake stake "migration" that moved real LP tokens to an arbitrary address.
- Root cause: `migrateStake()` contained no check that the `oldStaking` parameter was a valid staking contract. An attacker could supply a contract with the same `oldStaking` parameter, specifying an arbitrary deposit amount and a recipient address, and the function would dutifully migrate the tokens.
- Mechanics: The attacker stood up a contract claiming an arbitrary staked amount and destination, then called `migrateStake()`; ~320k Stax FRAX/TEMPLE LP tokens (~$2.3M) were moved into the attacker's contract and swapped for ETH inside it. Attack tx 0x8c3f442f…, attacker 0x9c9fb3100a2a521985f0c47de3b4598dafd25b01 — funded via Binance shortly before, with funds then forwarded to 0x2b63d4a3b2db8acbb2671ea7b16993077f1db5a0 where they remained. Loss was small relative to TempleDAO's ~$57M TVL; Spreek raised the alarm about an hour after the exploit and STAX confirmed.
- Lesson: (1) Any migrate/claim function that accepts a caller-supplied staking contract must validate that contract against a registry/whitelist and must not trust caller-supplied deposit amounts or recipients — grep every `migrate`/`migrateStake` path in staking protocols. (2) The contract was published in June and exploited in October: a "worryingly simple" oversight sat live for 4 months — add every function taking a contract-address parameter to the audit checklist, not just the happy path.
- REKT narrative extras: rekt called the hack "worryingly simple" (credit FrankResearcher, BlockSec); the day before, DCF GOD told the community "after a year of it, I'm low on hope". TempleDAO, an OHM-fork turned FRAX stablecoin farm, insisted its Core Vaults share no code with STAX and that "remediations will be made for all affected users".

## Terra (LUNA / UST / Anchor) — 2022-05-11 — multi-billion $ collapse
*source slug: terra-rekt (500) → resolved to luna-rekt*
- Chain/Attack vector: Terra blockchain — economic death spiral of the UST algorithmic stablecoin (not a smart-contract exploit): UST depeg → hyperinflationary LUNA mint/burn arbitrage → both assets spiral to zero.
- Root cause: The mint/burn arbitrage (burn $1 UST ↔ mint $1 of LUNA) can only defend the peg while LUNA's market cap stays above UST's and confidence holds; once bearish flows flipped the UST/LUNA market caps and demand collapsed, no minting speed could absorb the sell pressure — the classic algo-stable death spiral. Do Kwon had already failed once with Basis Cash (2020).
- Mechanics: First tremor on Saturday: UST briefly under $0.99 with $2.2B volume through the Curve UST pool; the withdrawal of 150M UST from Curve minutes earlier opened the window. Anchor deposits halved from >$14B to ~$6B, UST/LUNA market caps flipped, Degenbox loop liquidations cascaded, and Binance stopped selling UST below 0.70 while Terra withdrawals were suspended. UST bottomed at 0.67, "stabilised" near 0.90, then free-fell to 30–50 cents; LUNA lost >95% in 24h to below $2.5. The rescue plan (speed up LUNA minting for arbitrage) effectively sentenced LUNA to hyperinflation; LFG's BTC warchest ($750M loaned to market makers) could not hold the peg.
- Lesson: (1) For any algorithmic stablecoin, track the single kill metric — reserve-token market cap vs. stablecoin market cap, plus real redeemable backing: when the ratio approaches 1.0 in a falling market, the spiral is arithmetically unstoppable regardless of "defense" mechanisms or BTC warchests. (2) Audit contagion concentration: protocols whose treasuries or user deposits sit in one algo-stable yield venue (Anchor-style 20%, Degenbox >100% loops) all die together — USDN and Tron began depegging within days.
- REKT narrative extras: rekt: "The dark horseman has arrived… There will be a before and an after from this day"; MakerDAO's Rune had called UST a "solid ponzi" in Dec 2021; Kwon's taunts ("cockroaches", "amusing morning") aged badly; deliberate-attack and regulator-conspiracy theories noted but unproven at write time.

## THORChain — 2021-07-16 — $5M
- Chain/Attack vector: THORChain's ETH Bifrost (bridge) on Ethereum — deposit-value spoofing via an "override loop" reached by wrapping the router with an attacker contract.
- Root cause: The ETH Bifrost code contained an override loop designed only for `vaultTransferEvent` transactions; the hacker wrapped the router with their own contract to access that override. A transaction sent with call value 0 and deposit amount 0 caused the override loop to read the msg.value (set to 200) as `txvalue()`, crediting a fake deposit.
- Mechanics: The exploit was looped to drain the bridge's liquidity across multiple assets: 2,500 ETH, 57,975 SUSHI, 8.7 YFI, 171,912 DODO, 514.5 ALCX, 1,167,216 KYL, 13.3 AAVE — ~$5M total. The code even carried the comment "// it is important to keep this part outside the above loop"; the official fix was "make the over-ride only happen if it specifically is a vaultTransferEvent". Attacker wallet 0x3a196410a0f5facd08fd7880a4b8551cd085c031, with funds sent through Tornado.
- Lesson: (1) In bridge/router code, transaction-type discrimination must be explicit (calldata signature match, whitelist) — wrapping the router with your own contract must never be able to reach privileged value-transfer overrides; hunt for loops whose branch semantics change which value/deposit field gets read. (2) A code comment marking exactly where logic "must stay outside the loop" is a free audit beacon — such comments flag fragile invariants that deserve an adversarial test.
- REKT narrative extras: THORChain's market cap had peaked near $5B two months earlier ($1.2B at write time), so rekt framed it as "even the old gods cannot escape the exploits"; Cronje's maxim quoted: "innovation leads to exploitation"; the team published a preliminary incident report the same day.

## Transit Swap — 2022-10-02 — $21M (~70% returned)
*source slug: transit-rekt (500) → resolved to transit-swap-rekt*
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum + BSC; arbitrary-address token drain from every user who had approved Transit Swap's swap contracts — a controllable `transferFrom` external call inside unverified contracts.
- Root cause: In the closed-source swap contracts, the `claimTokens` function executed an external `transferFrom` (selector 0x23b872dd) whose token address and from/to/amount parameters were all attacker-controllable (SlowMist decompiled the bytecode to confirm), letting anyone pull any approved token straight to the exploiter.
- Mechanics: First attack tx just after 18:30 UTC on Sunday; the draining ran ~30 minutes, then stolen tokens were swapped to ETH/BNB. Taken: 3,180 ETH ($4.2M, returned), 1,500 Binance-pegged ETH ($2M, returned), 50k BNB ($14M, $10.4M returned); 2,500 BNB (~$715k) went to Tornado Cash and ~$3.5M remained on the attacker's BSC address at write time. After SlowMist + Bitrace + Peckshield uncovered the hacker's IP, email and addresses, ~70% of funds were returned to 0xD989f7B4320c6e69ceA3d914444c19AB67D3a35E. Vulnerable contract users were told to revoke: 0xed1afc8c4604958c2f38a3408fa63b32e737c428 (ETH and BSC).
- Lesson: (1) In any swap/aggregator "claim/redeem" path, the token, from, and amount operands of internal `transferFrom` calls must derive from validated internal state, never from raw call data — decompile unverified routers and grep for 0x23b872dd with caller-controlled operands. (2) Unverified contracts + user approvals = full-wallet theft primitive: closed-source code makes DYOR impossible and hides this class of bug, so treat unverified router deployments as a standing hunting target.
- REKT narrative extras: over $1M of the proceeds was "lost in transit" to MEV bots ("not all MEV bots are down 0xbad"); rekt mused that the fast partial return smelled of an insider using privileged info before it "blows over".

## Treasure DAO — 2022-03-03 — ~$1.4M in NFTs
- Chain/Attack vector: Arbitrum; NFT marketplace logic bug — `buyItem` missing a quantity check let listed NFTs be "bought" for zero.
- Root cause: The marketplace's `buyItem` function did not check that quantity > 0. harry.eth: "buyItem doesn't check quantity is > 0... exploiter calls buyItem() with zero quantity, pays 0, still receives NFT". The one-line fix: `require(_quantity > 0, "Cannot buy zero")`.
- Mechanics: The attacker called `buyItem()` with quantity 0 on existing listings, paying nothing while receiving the NFTs — draining ~$1.4M worth of NFTs from the largest Arbitrum NFT marketplace (including Smol Brains listings with a ~2.5k MAGIC / >$9k floor). The marketplace was paused shortly after exploitation began; MAGIC dipped about a third before stabilising at ~-10%. On-chain investigators linked an exploiter account to ENS and Binance addresses, and the doxxing threat prompted many NFTs to be returned — ~50 remained stolen when the team promised to make buyers whole.
- Lesson: (1) Every NFT marketplace buy/offer path needs explicit quantity validation — zero-quantity purchases collapse the payment term regardless of price; `require(_quantity > 0)` is the single line that separates a marketplace from a free-mint machine. (2) When fees or payments are computed multiplicatively with quantity, fuzz the zero edge case across buy/bid/accept paths — this bug class recurs wherever quantity is a user input.
- REKT narrative extras: rekt's lede: "Swiggity swooty, somebody plundered the Treasure DAO booty"; co-founder John Patten: "I will personally give up all of my Smols to repair this"; the MAGIC community's near-instant rally saved the project.

## TMXTribe — 2026-01-08 — $1.4M
*source slug: tribe-rekt (500) → resolved to tmztribe-rekt*
- Chain/Attack vector: Arbitrum; logic bug in unverified contracts of a GMX-fork perps DEX — an exploit loop through mint/stake/swap mechanics that drained the protocol for 36 hours with no pause.
- Root cause: Flawed logic in the unverified contract's handling of LP minting, staking and swapping — no balance checks, no circuit breakers. The cycle "mint & stake TMX LP with USDT → swap the deposited USDT for USDG (protocol stablecoin) → unstake the LP → sell/drain USDG → repeat" could run indefinitely because each iteration looked like a valid transaction to the contract. No audit, unverified contracts.
- Mechanics: Defimon Alerts flagged the exploit on Jan 5; the exploiter 0x763a67E4418278f84c04383071fC00165C112661 (funded Jan 3) and a parallel secondary address 0x16Ed3AFf3255FDDB44dAa73B4dE06f0C2E15288d ran the identical loop across 36 hours and 502 transactions, converting drained assets to ETH in systematic batches. Funds were bridged to Ethereum via Across (260 WETH $821k, 3.42 WBTC $312k, 15.9 WETH $50k) and deposited into Tornado Cash (~$1.2M laundered). Meanwhile the team wallet kept deploying and upgrading contracts all day and sent an on-chain 80/20 bounty offer — but never paused; public comms stayed silent for days.
- Lesson: (1) Fork audits must diff inherited logic and prove the mint/stake/unstake cycle can't round-trip value out through an internal stablecoin (USDG) — an unverified, unaudited GMX fork with internal-stablecoin swap logic is a red-flag set; check for missing "staked LP can't be immediately unstaked/sold" guards. (2) Incident-response signature for hunters: "36 hours of draining with zero pause" means the protocol has no circuit breakers at all — when scouting forks, verify a pause mechanism exists and test whether the team can actually trigger it before depositing.
- REKT narrative extras: rekt: "Was this incompetence so profound it became indistinguishable from malice, or malice so brazen it disguised itself as incompetence?"; tracked live by CertiK, QuillAudits and Hacken's Extractor; the bounty message got only silence, and the original TMXTribe Twitter was deleted then reappeared with a phishing-flagged link.

## Uranium Finance — 2021-04-28 — ~$57M
- Chain/Attack vector: BSC; math bug in the Uniswap-v2-forked `UraniumPair` — a broken constant-product check let 1 wei drain 98% of a pair's output token.
- Root cause: While copying Uniswap v2, the team changed the constant 1,000 to 10,000 in two places but not at the end of the fee-adjusted `x * y = k` check, breaking the invariant verification before reserve balances were updated. bzX co-founder Kyle Kistner pointed out the diff that caused it.
- Mechanics: The attacker first sent the minimum amount of each token to the pair contracts, then used a low-level `swap()` to drain both reserves: 34k WBNB ($18M), 17.9M BUSD ($17.9M), 1.8k ETH ($4.7M), 80 BTC ($4.3M), 26.5k DOT ($0.8M), 638k ADA ($0.8M), 5.7M USDT ($5.7M) — ~$57.2M, with ≥2,200 ETH quickly mixed through Tornado Cash. The buggy v2 had been live ~10 days; the fix (v2.1, "the only significant change") was due to ship the same day the exploit hit, and the contracts repo was deleted from GitHub afterwards.
- Lesson: (1) Fork-diff audits: every numeric deviation from upstream AMM code invalidates the core invariant — diff all constants between fork and source before launch; a 1000→10000 change in two of three places breaks `x*y=k` silently. (2) "Bug introduced by the team's own edit + repo deleted + patched version due the same day" is the insider/rug signature — treat exploit timing that coincides with a scheduled fix migration as a red flag, not a coincidence.
- REKT narrative extras: second place on rekt's leaderboard at the time (a "much more preferable prize than the EASY tokens of our previous article"); their MasterChef rewards contract had already been exploited earlier that month; credit 0xdeadf4ce and igor igamberdiev; rekt asked openly whether the team added the bug and used the liquidity migration to hide it.

## Value DeFi — 2020-11-14 — $7M ($2M returned)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ethereum; flash-loan price manipulation of the Curve pool used as a spot oracle, plus a withdrawal path that did its math with the wrong Curve function.
- Root cause: Value DeFi priced vault shares from the Curve spot price; the attacker flash-loaned to move that price, and the withdrawal (step 7 of the attack) used the wrong Curve function for the math — withdrawing far more than the position was worth. The team didn't realise withdrawals were possible not only through the main Bank contract but also from the Vault through its Proxy.
- Mechanics: At 15:24 — 20 minutes before a scheduled AMA — the attacker manipulated the Curve pool price with a flash loan and withdrew through the Vault-proxy path, draining ~$7M from the vault (TVL had been >$11M earlier in the day). $2M plus a note were returned; the VALUE token fell from $2.73 to $1.87. It followed $FARM and $AKRO as the third flash-loan victim in quick succession.
- Lesson: (1) Never derive deposit/withdrawal value from a Curve spot price — use virtual price / TWAP, and specifically audit WHICH getter each withdrawal math calls, because the "wrong Curve function" converts overpriced shares into real funds. (2) Enumerate every withdrawal entry point: a safe "Bank" contract means nothing if a second Vault-proxy path reaches the same funds with different checks — map all proxy paths into shared value.
- REKT narrative extras: the team had tweeted security boasts a day before the hack (deleted, but rekt kept the screenshot — "PRICELESS"); Stani Kulechov tweeted as the AMA began; rekt's closing essay: flash loans "are here to teach and humble anyone who rushes this process".

## Venus Protocol & Blizz Finance — 2022-05-13 — $13.5M (Venus) + $8.3M (Blizz)
*source slug: venus-rekt (500) → resolved to venus-blizz-rekt*
- Chain/Attack vector: BSC (Venus Protocol) + Avalanche (Blizz Finance); LUNA-collateral lending drained after the Chainlink oracle's hardcoded price floor kept valuing crashing LUNA at $0.10.
- Root cause: Both lending protocols valued LUNA collateral through a Chainlink feed whose `minAnswer` for LUNA was hardcoded at $0.10; once LUNA collapsed below that floor (post-UST death spiral), attackers bought near-worthless LUNA at market price and deposited it as $0.10-valued collateral to borrow out the platforms. Neither protocol had automated circuit breakers in place.
- Mechanics: After the failed UST recovery plan crashed LUNA from an ATH of ~$120 to fractions of a cent, anyone could buy large LUNA amounts at market and borrow against the stale $0.10 valuation. Venus (~$1B TVL) suspended activity in time and committed its Risk Fund to remedy the ~$13.5M shortfall; Blizz Finance could not react because of its governance timelock and was wiped out — $8.3M of TVL lent out against worthless collateral. Chainlink's response described its automatic circuit-breaker docs and best practices, while rekt faulted both sides: the feed parameters should have been updated, and protocols need their own automated pause triggers.
- Lesson: (1) `minAnswer`/`maxAnswer` floors are stale-price primitives: audit every price feed for hardcoded bounds and cross-check them against the collateral's realistic crash range — any floor above zero on a volatile collateral becomes a borrow-against-garbage machine in a crash. (2) A pause mechanism gated behind a governance timelock is no pause at all — Blizz died waiting out its timelock; audit the response-path latency (who can pause, and how fast) rather than just checking a pause exists.
- REKT narrative extras: "The Luna fall-out continues" — the first lending casualties of the Terra implosion; alarms were reportedly raised in advance but preventative measures came too late; rekt closed by noting the still-unaccounted $3B of BTC held by the Luna Foundation Guard.

## Venus Protocol (Rekt IV) — 2026-03-18 — $3.7M extracted / $2.15M bad debt
*source slug: venus2-rekt (500) → resolved to venus-protocol-rekt4*
- Chain/Attack vector: BNB Chain; Mango-Markets-style price manipulation of the THE market, amplified by a donation attack that bypassed the supply cap — a Compound V2 fork `mint()` enforcement gap.
- Root cause: Venus's supply cap is enforced only inside the `mint()` deposit path; raw ERC-20 `transfer()` calls sent directly to the vTHE contract bypass it entirely (donation attack). Donated tokens still count in `balanceOf` and inflate the vToken exchange rate (3.81×), multiplying the value of existing collateral the cap was meant to bound. The mechanism was documented in Venus's own Code4rena 2023 audit (finding M-10) and dismissed as "supported behavior with no negative side effects"; the same vector hit Venus's ZKSync deployment in Feb 2025 ($717k net bad debt) and the BNB core pool was never patched.
- Mechanics: From June 2025, a wallet funded with 7,447 ETH (~$16.29M) via 77 Tornado Cash transfers deposited ETH on Aave, borrowed ~$9.92M in stables, and spent nine months accumulating 84% of Venus's 14.5M THE supply cap. On Mar 15 it supplied 12.2M THE through normal deposits, then six wallets donated ~36M THE directly to vTHE (peak 53.2M THE = 367% of cap); a delegated-borrower contract ran a recursive loop — borrow CAKE/BNB/BTCB/USDC → buy THE on thin DEX liquidity (spot $0.26 → ~$4) → donate more THE → wait for the resilient/TWAP oracle to accept ~$0.51. Peak borrow ~$14.9M across ~50 transactions; the position liquidated when the market stopped moving, 53.2M THE crashed to $0.22, and $2.15M in bad debt remained (1.18M CAKE + 1.84M THE). The extracted $5.07M (2,172 BNB, 1.516M CAKE, 20 BTCB) sits unmixed in the attacker's wallet — on-chain P&L is negative, so a CEX THE-perp short is the active theory.
- Lesson: (1) In every Compound V2 fork, the supply cap must be enforced on ALL token entry paths, not just `mint()` — direct donations inflate `exchangeRate` and bypass caps (Hacken: "verify whether direct token transfers to your cToken contracts bypass supply-cap logic"); hunt for exchange-rate math based on raw `balanceOf` that doesn't distinguish donated from minted tokens. (2) Dismissed audit findings are hunting gold: a Code4rena finding labelled "no negative side effects" plus a real exploit on a sibling deployment equals a still-open live bug elsewhere in the same codebase.
- REKT narrative extras: researcher William Li, who modeled this exact attack in a 2023 paper, flagged it in real time and publicly shorted THE for a $15k profit; Venus paused THE borrows ~3h after his alert; the BoundValidator actually rejected the spiked price for ~37 minutes before feeds converged; it's Venus's fourth rekt in five years ($95M bad debt 2021 XVS pump, $14.2M May 2022 LUNA crash, $717k ZKSync Feb 2025) — and the post-mortem concedes "this is a gap in our code we are working to close".
