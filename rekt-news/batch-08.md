# REKT.news knowledge cards — batch-08

## Poloniex — 2023-11-10 — $126M (CEX hot wallets)
- Chain/Attack vector: CEX hot-wallet drain across three chains (Ethereum $59.2M, TRON $48.6M, BTC $18.6M per Arkham); off-chain vector — phishing/employee-device compromise to extract private keys, no smart-contract flaw.
- Root cause: Compromised hot-wallet keys of Justin Sun's Poloniex exchange. The attack began 10:30 AM UTC with a 4,900 ETH (~$10M) drain from the address labelled "Poloniex 4" on Etherscan, then spread to TRON and BTC — a classic multi-chain, single-custody-layer key compromise.
- Mechanics: Largest single asset losses were 33M USDT (22M on TRON, 11M on ETH), $18.6M native BTC, a further $14M of BTC on TRON and $5M USDC on Ethereum. On Ethereum the loot was dispersed across ~13 labelled hacker addresses which swapped tokens to ETH and held or further dispersed. The attacker's swap strategy had side effects: converting the freezable TRON-side USDT to TRX pumped Justin Sun's TRX bags by 25%, and in the liquidation rush ~$2.6M of Golem's GLM was lost by transferring it directly to the token's contract. Poloniex announced the wallet was "disabled for maintenance" ~30 min after the drain began; Sun then promised full reimbursement and a 5% whitehat bounty with a 7-day grace period.
- Lesson: CEX incidents spanning multiple chains simultaneously point to one custody/ops compromise, not per-chain bugs — triage for key theft first. On-chain, the attacker's own "mistakes" (pumping a related token 25%, burning $2.6M GLM to the token contract, visible dispersal chains) are triage telemetry: rushed conversion behavior and frozen-asset avoidance (USDT→TRX) mark a live drain in progress.
- REKT narrative extras: Arkham offered a $4,000 bounty for hacker ID via its intel market; rekt flags the possible DPRK/Lazarus link (over $250M lost that year in Lazarus-linked incidents: Atomic Wallet, AlphaPo, Stake, CoinEx). Came weeks after Sun's HTX lost $7.9M (returned within two weeks); attacker ETH 0x0a5984f86200415894821bfefc1c1de036dbf9e7, TRON TKK6d1YALy8HCSoCSWWd1ZJhyC9NPPx4wa, BTC bc1qnpc7u2ha7ct9c458rrqsawylz9e9j6jvkvzttt.

## Poly Network — 2021-08-10 — ~$611M (Ethereum + BSC + Polygon)
- Chain/Attack vector: Cross-chain bridge/relay privilege escalation on three chains via the EthCrossChainManager relay contract — no private-key compromise, no flash loan.
- Root cause: `EthCrossChainManager.verifyHeaderAndExecuteTx()` was callable by anyone and its `executeCrossChainTx` step would call ANY target contract, including the privileged `EthCrossChainData` contract itself, thereby passing the `onlyOwner` check. The function selector for the target call was derived from a user-supplied `_method` value, so the attacker only had to grind a 4-byte sighash prefix collision: `putCurEpochConPubKeyBytes(bytes)` = 0x41973cd9 vs crafted `f1121318093(bytes,bytes,uint64)` = 0x41973cd9.
- Mechanics: The attacker sent cross-chain messages to the Proxy Lock Contracts on Ethereum (0x250e7698...), BSC and Polygon that tricked EthCrossChainManager into calling EthCrossChainData and executing `putCurEpochConPubKeyBytes`, replacing the keeper public keys with attacker-controlled ones — the contract "hacked itself" (kelvinfichter's breakdown). With keeper control they drained Ethereum (96.4M USDC, 1,032 WBTC, 673k DAI, 43k UNI, 259.7B SHIB, 14.47 renBTC, 33.4M USDT, 26,109 wETH, 616k FEI), BSC (87.6M USDC, 26,629 BTCB, 6,613 BNB, 299 ETH, 1,023 BUSD) and Polygon (85M USDC). Tether froze all 33M USDT on Ethereum. After an OPSEC slip exposed by @WardBradt (a return tx sent from an address tied to FTX/Binance/OKEx accounts), the attacker announced "READY TO SURRENDER" and returned funds; at write time ~$2.6M (ETH), $1.1M (BSC) and $1M (Polygon) had been returned.
- Lesson: Cross-chain relay contracts must whitelist exactly which target contracts and functions a user message can invoke — the relay manager should never hold privileges over its own data/keeper contract ("separate concerns"). Never gate privileged calls on a 4-byte selector computed from user input: prefix collisions are trivially grindable, so use explicit target+function allowlists, not sighash matching.
- REKT narrative extras: At the time the biggest crypto hack ever — more than the entire rekt.news leaderboard combined. hanashiro.eth earned a 13.37 ETH tip from the hacker for USDT-handling advice and donated it to Infura, Etherscan and rekt.news; Poly Network's open letter and the hacker's DAO-distribution idea preceded the surrender. Analysis credited to SlowMist, BlockSec and breadcrumbs.app.

## Qubit Finance — 2022-01-27 — $80M (200k BNB)
- Chain/Attack vector: BSC lending protocol (PancakeBunny team) — cross-chain collateral mint without backing: QBridge deposit logic flaw, exploited straight from a Tornado-Cash-funded Ethereum address.
- Root cause: When `depositETH` was added to the QBridge, the legacy `deposit` function was left in the contract with its `tokenAddress` replaced by the zero address (ETH's "token address"). `tokenAddress.safeTransferFrom(depositer, address(this), amount)` on address(0) did not revert, so the deposit succeeded regardless of amount — Certik: "the deposit function was a function that should not be used after depositETH was newly developed, but it remained in the contract."
- Mechanics: At ~21:30 UTC on Jan 27 the attacker called the Ethereum-side QBridge deposit with no ETH locked, and the zero-address transfer silently succeeded. The BSC side credited them 77,162 qXETH (~$185M) of collateral against loans on Qubit, which they used to borrow WETH, BTC-B, USD stablecoins plus CAKE, BUNNY and MDX, swapping everything into 200k BNB (~$80M) that remained sitting in the BSC address. The cross-chain collateral feature had been audited by Theori in Dec 2021.
- Lesson: When adding a new asset/deposit path, the deprecated one must be removed or explicitly reverted — leftover legacy functions stay permanently callable and drift out of any audit scope. Also treat calls to address(0) as a hard failure everywhere (a call to a no-code address returns success in Solidity), and prove on the destination chain that minted credit strictly equals assets locked on the source chain.
- REKT narrative extras: #7 on the leaderboard at the time; the Qubit team (behind repeat offender PancakeBunny) signaled willingness to negotiate beyond their $250k Immunefi max bounty. rekt's grudge was personal: the PancakeBunny hacker had gifted them 100k DAI in May 2021, which they returned with the note "not to lose it this time".

## Raydium — 2022-12-16 — ~$4.4M (LP protocol fees)
- Chain/Attack vector: Solana AMM; compromised private key of the pool owner/admin account (suspected trojan) — no on-chain exploit, pure key/authority compromise.
- Root cause: The pool owner account held authority over Raydium's pools including the `withdraw_pnl` instruction, which the attacker invoked to drain accumulated trading/protocol fees without holding or burning any LP tokens; they also flipped the `SyncNeedTake` parameter to inflate expected fees and withdraw extra.
- Mechanics: The drain was first flagged by the Solana DEX aggregator PRISM ("a wallet is draining LP Pools from Raydium liquidity pools using admin wallet as a signer without having/burning LP tokens"), which pulled its own PRISM/USDC liquidity as a warning. Affected pools: SOL-USDC, SOL-USDT, RAY-USDC, RAY-USDT, RAY-SOL, stSOL-USDC, ZBC-USDC, UXP-USDC, whETH-USDC. Raydium's announcement came ~40 minutes later, halting authority on the AMM & farm programs and shipping a patch. Most funds were bridged to Ethereum, swapped to ETH and deposited into Tornado Cash, with 100k SOL (~$1.4M) still at the attacker's Solana address (AgJddDJLt17nHyXDCpyGELxwsZZQPqfUsuwzoiqVGJwD).
- Lesson: An AMM's admin/owner key is a direct fee-drain primitive — inventory every instruction the owner key can call (withdraw_pnl, fee-parameter setters like SyncNeedTake) and require multisig/derived-PDA custody for anything that moves accumulated protocol value. Watch for admin-signer transactions that touch fee accounts without corresponding LP-token accounting: that signature (drain "without having/burning LP tokens") is exactly what the aggregator PRISM caught in minutes.
- REKT narrative extras: Analysis by OtterSec; rekt's post-FTX framing notes the eternal ambiguity of "compromised key" cases — insider opportunism can't be ruled out. The incident hit during the bear-market fallout from FTX's collapse, which rekt highlights as context for tempted ecosystem devs.

## Ronin Network — 2022-03-23 (detected 2022-03-29) — ~$624M (173,600 ETH + 25.5M USDC)
- Chain/Attack vector: Ronin (Axie Infinity) Ethereum sidechain bridge; validator-threshold compromise — four Sky Mavis validator keys plus a never-revoked Axie DAO delegation, no smart-contract bug.
- Root cause: Ronin's PoA bridge had 9 validators and a 5-of-9 approval threshold, but Sky Mavis operated 4 of them — one breach short of consensus. In Nov 2021 the Axie DAO had approved Sky Mavis validators to sign on its behalf for a gas-free RPC node during peak AXS traffic; the arrangement ended in December but the whitelist was never revoked, handing the attacker the needed 5th signature once Sky Mavis's four validators were compromised.
- Mechanics: With 5-of-9 approval the attacker authorized two withdrawals from the Ronin Bridge contract: first 173,600 ETH, then 25.5M USDC (swapped to ETH via other addresses and returned to the main wallet). Detection took six days — the alert came from a user who couldn't withdraw 5k ETH. Funds sat at 0x098b716b8aaf21512996dc57eb0615e2383e2f96; 6,250 ETH was peeled off, some moved to FTX and Crypto.com, and the wallet was originally funded from a Binance account ("KYC'd accounts are easily acquired").
- Lesson: Bridge security must assume correlated compromise of any single operator group — thresholds should be counted in *distinct operators*, not validators, and any cross-party signing delegation needs an expiry that is enforced on-chain, because "temporary" grants never revoke themselves. The six-day detection gap is the other lesson: monitor bridge balances and withdrawals continuously — a $624M outflow with zero alarms is a monitoring failure, not just a key failure.
- REKT narrative extras: New biggest crypto hack ever at the time, dethroning Poly Network; nobody noticed for six days. Sky Mavis moved the threshold to 8-of-9 — but did so ~11 hours *before* officially announcing the incident. The case is rekt's exhibit A that decentralisation is not academic: validator-set centralisation turned one operator's breach into the entire bridge's bank run.
