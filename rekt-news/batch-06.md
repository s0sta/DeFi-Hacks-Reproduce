## Levana Protocol — 2023-12-29 — ~$1.1M (~10% of protocol liquidity)
- Chain/Attack vector: Osmosis (Cosmos) / oracle manipulation — opening high-leverage perps positions at deliberately stale oracle prices, then forcing the price update through in the same tx.
- Root cause: Levana priced leveraged positions off an oracle that updated only via user trades, the off-chain update bot, or every 90 seconds; the protocol accepted new positions against a stale price with no requirement that a fresh oracle update accompany the position, so an attacker who jammed the update channel (spam + DDoS) could open positions at prices they knew were outdated and directionally guaranteed to move. An Osmosis fee-market bug that made the bot's gas insufficient during congestion widened the stale windows.
- Mechanics: The attack began Dec 13 and ramped up on Dec 26, running ~14 days. Stage 1 drained ~4% of LPs over 12 days with tiny per-trade profits (attributed to organic trader PnL at first); stage 2, during Osmosis congestion, drained an additional ~5%. Total loss ~$1.146M: stATOM_USD $241k, ATOM_USD $229k, BTC_USD $190k, ETH_USD $128k, TIA_USD $108k, USDC pools $168k + $82k. The attacker spammed txs so no oracle updates could land, DDoSed the backend scheduled to push updates, tracked the delta between stale oracle data and real market price, then used a multiexecute tx to open a long/short AND push the stale data to market pricing in one atomic action — profiting with certainty. The team noticed only when losses spiked during congestion and paused the protocol.
- Lesson: (1) Every position open must consume a FRESH oracle price — require the update in the same tx or queue positions behind an update; anything else turns "jammed update channel" into free money. (2) Audit the failure mode "oracle updates stop arriving" (fee-market bugs, bot liveness, congestion, DDoS), not just price manipulation of the oracle itself.
- REKT narrative extras: Audited by FYEO and Peckshield, but congestion-dependent bugs were out of scope; a concurrent DDoS hampered the team's response. Levana plans to make users whole via fees and an LVN airdrop, and to add a tx queuing system requiring a fresh oracle price to open positions.

## Level Finance — 2023-05-02 — ~$1.1M (214k LVL → 3,345 BNB)
- Chain/Attack vector: BSC / repeated referral reward claims — LevelReferralControllerV2 `claimMultiple` allowed the same epoch's referral rewards to be claimed over and over.
- Root cause: `claimMultiple` had no check that a claim's epoch was not being reused (no per-user epoch-claimed tracking / replay guard), so the referral reward ledger was effectively an unlimited mint. The vulnerable implementation was introduced in an April 18 proxy upgrade whose source was never committed to the public GitHub repo.
- Mechanics: The exploiter created many referral accounts, used flash loans to make swaps and inflate their reward tier, then repeatedly called `claimMultiple` for the same epoch, draining 214k LVL (~$1.1M) which they dumped for 3,345 BNB. The LVL price crashed from $8.42 to $2.93 (-65%) before mostly recovering. The attack was first attempted over a week earlier but went unnoticed; definalist's monitoring raised the alarm while the exploit was ongoing, and the Level team confirmed two hours later.
- Lesson: (1) Every reward/rebate claim path must record claimed epochs (or nonce) per user — missing epoch-replay protection on a claim function is an infinite mint. (2) For proxy contracts, audit the DEPLOYED bytecode behind the proxy, not the audited GitHub commit — post-audit upgrades are the most common source of unaudited code (Quantstamp confirmed the vuln post-dated their audit).
- REKT narrative extras: Quantstamp and Obelisk had audited the core contracts without spotting it; Quantstamp later stated the bug was introduced in the April 18 upgrade (proxy 0x977087422C008233615b572fBC3F209Ed300063a, impl 0x9f00…) not present in the audited commit. Losses were contained to the referral programme — Treasury funds and LPs were safe. Exploiter: 0x70319d1c09e1373fc7b10403c852909e5b20a9d5.
